**What revision 23 changes (D210)**
- **R4 and R54.** A rule table that is not well formed is refused as a whole, naming at least one fault its loader finds. Not well formed means a key given more than once, a value YAML cannot build, a pattern that does not compile or cannot be used, a value of the wrong kind for its field, or fields that contradict one another. Naming every fault, or every combination of repeated values, is now outside R4. The replaced clauses are those revisions 19 to 22 added for malformed values, unusable patterns and repeated keys. A well-formed table is still checked rule by rule, and a changed v1 is still refused as changed first.
- **R42, R43 and P15.** Removing, renaming or replacing a case lock file while the case is in use, and interrupting a save at an arbitrary instant, are outside the guarantees. Under them the tool reports as it can, and a check made after the interference stops gives the true answer.
- **No code change.** I read verify-104 to verify-119. The two latest majors fall outside the new scope:
  - verify-118 (T12 at 43332c6) needs both a replaced lock file and a KeyboardInterrupt.
  - verify-119 (T03 at 3ae10cc) is a table that is not well formed, and its refusal still names at least one fault.

**Two readings I made and put to the owner as questions (defaults written in)**
1. I read "reports as it can" as also covering R42's and P15's two guarantees under interference from revision 13. These are "never intact when two saves told recorded share a position" and "an intact log holds every save told recorded". They now describe what the tool tries.
2. I read "a nested save from a handler" as a way of interrupting the outer save. So R42 still guarantees that the nested save is refused before it writes anything, and only what the interrupted save then does is out of scope.
