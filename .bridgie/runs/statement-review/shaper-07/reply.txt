Revision 7 of the statement-review spec is drafted below, following D79; nothing else in the repo was touched. It includes the owner's T03 and T12 work since revision 6 and the verify-41 and verify-42 reviews.

**What changed (every id, form and risk tag is kept)**
- **R4: who can settle an ambiguous credit.** Only a per-line override or an own-account pairing settles it. A case-profile name found in the credit's wording now only adds its bucket as one more candidate, because a bank line can't tell the payer from someone named in the payment reference. That is the verify-41 finding: a director named in ACME LTD's payment reference settled the credit as director funding.
- **R4: cheque wording.** It is now named beside the payment rail as a payment-method cue with no bucket, so a cheque with cash paid-in wording is ambiguous by R4's own text (D76).
- **R4: names clause.** I reworded it to apply "when no line-type cue points to a money-in bucket". The old wording ("with no line-type cue") would have let the rail on every Faster Payment, and now a cheque, stop two disagreeing name lists from making a credit ambiguous. The design has meant the new reading since revision 3, and T03 already builds it.
- **R5 and R6** change only to say a profile name adds a candidate to an ambiguous credit and settles nothing.
- **Design, categoriser:** the profile name drops below the ambiguous verdict in the precedence. Both profile candidates are recorded when both a director's and an own account's name match, and the judgement queue shows the bucket each would give.
- **Design, case log (T12):** this covers the gap verify-42 found, using D74 and D75 as already decided. A reader tries to take the lock first, so it reports "intact" only after finding the case free. A writer waits a moment for a reader's check instead of being refused at once. A lock that can't be tested is reported as such, not blamed on another window. This describes what the T12 worktree already does; R43 and P15 are unchanged.

**What the next builds need to fix**
- **T03:**
  - It still adds only one profile candidate when both names match.
  - Its `README.md:78` still says the profile settles an ambiguous credit.
  - The table validator still accepts `(?i)INV[0-9]+`, the fifth review in a row to find it.

**Decision for you**
- **R5 and R6 against protected wording.** R5 and R6 don't say that a protected verdict (a line-type cue, or lender, loan or director wording) outranks a profile name. The design has done that since revision 3 and T03 tests it. So REFUND JANE DOE, with Jane Doe a director, is a refund, not director funding. T05's reviewer will judge R5 by its text. The design default is to state the precedence in R5 and R6 next revision.

In FROM POT TRANSFER CASH DEPOSIT the own-pot rule matches both FROM POT and POT TRANSFER, and the longer POT TRANSFER takes in TRANSFER, so no payment rail is left over and the own-account cue decides.\n  - *The verdict on a credit*, in this order:\n    1. Collect every credit rule that matches, from both stages, at every position where its wording occurs, overlapping occurrences included. A match inside a longer match from another rule counts only as the longer one (STRIPE inside STRIPE CAPITAL, LOAN inside DIRECTOR LOAN, REFUND inside HMRC REFUND, TRANSFER inside POT TRANSFER). What remains are the credit's matches.\n    2. A returned-payment code the bank prints at the start of the line settles the line type on its own, because it is the bank's code, not payer wording.\n    3. A payment-method cue (the rail or a cheque) together with cash paid-in wording makes the credit ambiguous, whatever other cue matches (BACS CREDIT HMRC CASH PAID IN; CHQ CASH DEPOSIT): the line says both that the money came another way and that it came as cash.\n    4. Otherwise, if any cue with a bucket matches, the cues decide: cues in one bucket give that bucket, and cues in two or more buckets make the credit ambiguous.\n    5. With no cue that points to a bucket (R4's 'when no line-type cue points to a money-in bucket'), the names decide in the same way, whether or not a payment-method cue matched (FPS CREDIT STRIPE GRANT and CHQ STRIPE GRANT are ambiguous). A payment-method cue alone decides nothing. If no name matches either, nothing is decided, and the line falls through the precedence below to the profile, firm rules and the default.\n    6. Inside the bucket decided, the category is the first in the table's declared category order that a deciding match carries: in trading receipts, cash deposit, then customer transfer, then card settlement; in refunds and returned payments, returned payment, then refund. Every other bucket has one category. The rule recorded is the first deciding match, in table order, that carries that category.\n    7. An ambiguous credit's table candidates are all of its matches from step 1, from both stages, payment-method cues included, written in table order. Its category is other (unexplained). The profile's candidates join them at precedence step (4).\n  - *Bare words are not evidence* (D48): SAVINGS, SAVER, POT, SPACE or a pot label such as TAX POT without move wording, and CAPITAL outside capital introduced, injected or contributed, match no list.\n  - *No hidden tie-breaks* (revision 6): a rule never carries an exception that hides another list's cue or name, and since D70 no v2 rule carries an exception at all: the cash rule's 'unless cheque' went when the cheque became a cue. Where two lists' wording meets, either a longer phrase goes on one list, or the credit is ambiguous.\n  - *Table validation* (revision 6): a table is refused at load, naming the rule, when a pattern compiles on its own but not in the form the matcher compiles it in (a global inline flag such as (?i)INV[0-9]+), so a table never loads and then fails on the first credit it matches.\n  - *Profile names* (revision 7, D78): R5 and R6 speak of a credit whose counterparty matches a name. A bank line has no separate payer field, so a profile name, like a listed name, matches wherever its words occur in the description, under the same whole-word and separator rules, and the description cannot tell the payer from a payment reference that names someone else. So a profile name decides a credit only when the table finds its wording unambiguous, and only below a protected verdict, as since revision 3 (owner question: R5 and R6 do not say the second); on an ambiguous credit it adds its bucket as one more candidate and settles nothing. A credit that both a director's and an own account's name match is director funding when the table does not find it ambiguous (R5 before R6, both non-trading), and adds both candidates when it does.\n  - *Precedence*, highest first: (1) a per-line override; (2) a protected verdict, meaning the line-type cues plus lender, director, capital-introduced, shareholder and generic loan and advance wording (R21's patterns and the cues); (3) an own-account pairing (R8), matched by amount and date across the case's accounts, not by text; (4) an ambiguous verdict, which puts the credit in unexplained money in with every candidate recorded: its table candidates from step 7, then one more candidate for each kind of case-profile name its wording matches, a director or shareholder (profile:director) before an own account (profile:own_account), settling nothing (R4, R5, R6; D78); (5) a case-profile name (R5, R6) on a line the table does not find ambiguous, a director or shareholder before an own account; (6) firm rules, which therefore never reach a line settled at (2) to (5) (R21, R4); (7) an unprotected verdict: the trading lists, invoice references, cash paid in and explained non-trading words; (8) the default, where a debit is supplier and a credit is unexplained. Revision 7 moves the profile name from above the ambiguous verdict to below it and keeps every other relative order of revision 6. A debit is never ambiguous, so a profile name on a debit (R6) is read at (5).\n  - Cash paid in is not protected (revision 2 never listed it), so a director named in the profile outranks cash wording, and a director's cash is director funding (R5), unless the table finds the credit ambiguous (cash wording with a payment-method cue, or with a second bucket's name), when the director's name is one more candidate. Every protected verdict, and every category a profile name or a pairing gives, is non-trading. So the only routes into trading receipts are an unambiguous trading verdict, a firm rule on a line that steps (2) to (5) did not settle, and a person's override.\n  - *What C1 hands on* (revision 4; revision 7): each line's category and rule, an ambiguous credit's conflict form and candidates included, for any rule id of a table the category that rule sets (none for a payment-method cue), and for a profile candidate the category its kind sets (director_funding, own_account_transfer). C1 does not build the judgement queue; C2 assembles it and C5 shows it (R15).\n- **C2 Figures layer** (src/cma/metrics.py, new src/cma/indicators.py). Adds M17-M28, the monthly summary and the indicators, including the three cash indicators. cash_deposit is a trading-receipts category, so M2 and every figure built on it include cash, and M18 reports the cash inside M2. All sums and threshold comparisons are in integer pence. Each figure comes with its evidence and, for sums, the evidence total. A case whose buckets do not reconcile is refused. C2 also assembles the judgement queue as a pure function of the categorised lines, the pairing candidates and the indicators (R15): unexplained lines, ambiguous credits among them, possible own-account transfers and every flagged line, largest amount first. An ambiguous credit's entry carries its candidates, each with the category C1 says its rule or profile kind sets and the bucket that category maps to.\n- **C3 Case store** (new src/cma/case.py). Case root, case folders, profile, override log, the hash-chained case log with its head record and single-writer lock, snapshot hash, purge and purge ledger, and the git-ignore and sync-folder checks.\n  - *Concurrency scope* (revision 5; R42, R43, P15): one writer at a time holds a case, whether the writers are windows, commands, threads or separate CaseLog objects in one process. A writer that finds the case held waits a moment, about as long as a reader's check holds the lock, and is then refused; it never queues behind another writer (revision 7). One whose expected log position is stale gets a conflict and reloads (R17). The lock belongs to a thread of a process, not to an object, and is recognised by the lock file itself, so two paths to one case folder, a symlink among them, are one case. The operating system drops the lock when its holder dies, so a crash never leaves a case locked.\n  - *What a reader reports* (revision 6, D74; revision 7 after verify-42; R43, P15): whoever holds the case, sign-off and every append included, reads once and judges the whole log: a changed, removed or reordered entry is broken and an incomplete last entry is incomplete, each named. Any other reader first tries to take the case's lock, a few times over a short pause, because another reader's check holds it only a moment. If it gets the lock, the case was free: it reads once holding it and judges the whole log, and this is the only way a reader that does not already hold the case reports intact (D75). If another writer holds the case, the reader judges only a read across which the head record did not change: something wrong at or before the recorded entries is broken, named; otherwise it reports the recorded entries it judged and the rest as being saved, never intact and never broken, whether or not anything follows them yet; with no such read within its retries, it reports the case as being saved and claims nothing about any entry. A lock the reader's own thread holds through another handle or path is not another holder, so such a reader reads once and judges the whole log. A lock that cannot be taken or tested is not another holder either (D50): the reader believes a steady read as it stands, an incomplete entry reported as incomplete, and with no steady read it reports that the log could not be checked because the lock could not be tested, claims nothing about any entry and names no other window. No writer state is read to decide any of this: the lock file holds no data.\n  - *Not supported, stated rather than detected*: forking a process while it holds a case's lock (the tool never forks; a forked child's writes through the inherited CaseLog are refused, and the child leaving a writer block releases nothing of its parent's); and case folders on a network share, where the operating system may emulate the lock per process and so not separate two CaseLog objects in one process (D66).\n- **C4 Importers** (src/cma/importers/). A shared step that walks and verifies the balance, extracted from monzo.py; new OBIE v3.1, TrueLayer and bank-CSV importers. Every importer writes the five-column file that ingest.load_csv validates.\n- **C5 Review workspace** (new src/cma/review/). A loopback server and one bundled page with no build step. For an ambiguous credit it shows each candidate and the bucket it would give: a table rule by its list, a payment-method candidate as giving no bucket, and a profile candidate as a name from the case profile with the bucket its kind gives, so the underwriter's override starts from the candidates. The drill-down shows each line's rule as recorded, which for an ambiguous credit is the conflict form (R16). While another window is saving, the case view says the case is being saved; it shows a broken-log warning only for something wrong at or before the recorded entries, or for an incomplete entry once it can judge the whole log (R43).\n- **C6 Preview, pack and sign-off** (new src/cma/pack.py). Memo field map, deterministic section text, the labelled policy-check section, grounding guard, HTML record, evidence JSON v1, monthly CSV, sign-off binding to the preview's snapshot, and an atomic write of the pack once, at sign-off. There is no timing setting (D32).\n- **C8 Verify** (new src/cma/verify.py). Recomputes an evidence record from its case and refuses records from another ruleset.\n- **C9 Pseudonymiser** (new src/cma/pseudonymise.py).\n- **C10 Synthetic data and evals** (src/cma/synth.py, evals/run_evals.py). rules and profile names share one whole-word definition and one separator set, so lists stop carrying spelling variants; the reader no longer reads writer marks. *Duplication introduced:* the v1 rule table, kept as data for one release. *No single-caller abstraction:* no storage interface for a future hosted version; no shared-handle registry for the case log; the label is one constant, not a templating layer; the importer contract has four callers; the rule-to-category lookup the queue uses is the table itself, plus two fixed profile kinds.\n- **Data-intensive.** *Idempotency:* imports are keyed by file hash, an identical override changes nothing, and a second sign-off is refused. *Determinism:* a credit's table verdict, category and table candidates are a pure function of its description, the sign of its amount and the tables with their category order; its profile candidates, and a profile settlement, add the case profile's names, which the snapshot hashes. Neither a credit's bucket nor its category depends on rule order within a stage, and only which of several same-category rules is recorded, and the order candidates are written in, follow table order. A debit's category follows table order by design. *Ordering and concurrency:* one writer at a time per case, across windows, commands, threads and CaseLog objects in one process, on a local disk; enforced by a lock plus the expected log position; a second tab is refused while the first writes, and gets a conflict and reloads if it writes from a stale position. A reader of a free case holds the lock for a moment and a writer waits that moment. While another writer holds the case, a reader judges only the recorded entries, from a read the head record held still across, and reports the rest as being saved. *Partial failure:* append-only logs with a check for a torn last entry; the pack is written to a temporary folder and then renamed. *Consistency:* the workspace reads the latest state; the preview and sign-off bind to a snapshot that includes the ruleset, the profile, thresholds, the policy hash and the show_policy_verdict setting, so a sign-off cannot attach to an outcome the underwriter did not see. *Migration:* additive, nothing to backfill. *Money:* integer pence, GBP only, thresholds compared in pence, and amounts with more than two decimals are refused.\n\n## Data changes\n\n**Category enum (src/cma/models.py).** Six values are added and none of the existing values change. A writer appends inside the case's writer lock and states the log position it last saw; it waits a moment for a reader's brief hold, is refused while another writer holds the case, and gets a conflict if its position is stale. A reader gets one of five answers: the whole log intact, with its head and entries, only from a reader that holds the case or found it free; the first bad or incomplete entry with its reason; the case being saved, with the recorded entries it judged and the rest not yet checked; the case being saved with nothing claimed; or, when the lock cannot be taken or tested and no read held still, not checked, with the lock error named and nothing claimed. Whatever needs the whole log intact (an append, a sign-off) decides holding the case, and no caller presents being saved or not checked as intact or as broken. Messages name entries by number and never quote their content. No caller forks while it holds the lock, and every caller keeps case folders on a local disk.\n\n**Review workspace API.** Internal to the package; the page and the server ship together, so it is not a shared contract. The case summary carries the case log's state as one of the case log contract's answers, so a second window says the case is being saved rather than warning of a broken log. A line in the judgement queue carries its rule and, for an ambiguous credit, its candidates, each with the category its rule or profile kind sets and the bucket that category maps to, or no bucket for a payment-method cue (R15). A line in a drill-down carries its rule as recorded (R16). The firm-rule preview counts the lines the rule matches and will not apply to, because a protected or ambiguous verdict, or a profile name, settled them (R20, R21, R4). Every request carries the session token. Every write carries the expected log position, gets a conflict if that position is stale, and is refused while another writer holds the case.\n\n**Pack.** memo.md has the sections in field-map order, figures with their ids, and blanks left for the underwriter. The workspace listens only on loopback, requires a random per-launch token on every request, rejects any Host header that is not the loopback address (against DNS rebinding) and sends no permissive CORS headers. Writes also need the expected log position.\n- **The writer lock is not access control** (revision 5). It orders honest writers on one machine; access is the case root's owner-only permissions (R31), which `cma case check` relies on rather than checks. Someone with the operating-system user's rights can rewrite the log and its head record together, which the files alone cannot show. The sign-off and the pack pin the log head they saw (R22), so a later rewrite no longer matches a signed pack. A writer that keeps saving can keep other readers at being saved, but cannot make them report the log as intact or broken (revision 6); sign-off and every append judge the whole log while holding the case. A process that checks a case in a tight loop can make another window's save wait and then be refused (revision 7); that takes the same user's rights, which already allow worse.\n- **Trust boundary:** bank data the applicant controls enters through the importers, and the loader checks its structure and balance chain. Descriptions are written by payers: they are escaped in HTML under a strict content security policy with no inline script, formula characters are neutralised in CSV, and they never go into prompts. The candidate rule ids shown for an ambiguous credit, and their buckets, come from the shipped tables and the two fixed profile kinds, not from payer text. Case-log messages name entries by number and never quote their content.\n- **Secrets:** an optional model API key in the environment, only when the offline lock is off. It is never logged or written to evidence.\n- **Personal data:** statements, the profile (director names, account identifiers), override reasons and packs stay in the case folder. Evidence holds ids, figures, rule ids and hashes, not descriptions or the profile; a profile candidate is recorded by its kind, never the name. Purge deletes the folder and leaves a ledger line with no personal data. Encryption at rest is the firm's disk encryption; the tool adds none of its own. Retention follows the firm's policy, and purge is how the tool carries it out.\n- **Risk path:** lending data (risk: data). The human acknowledgement is the named sign-off bound to the snapshot. The name is typed, not authenticated (README known limit), so the OS user is recorded alongside it. Real sign-in belongs to a hosted version. The illustrative outcome is computed, labelled and never signed as a decision.\n- **Worst cases with this deployed:** First, an applicant writes script into a payment reference to act in the underwriter's browser, for example to change categories or sign off. Escaping, the content security policy and the token stop it. Second, an applicant makes money look like trading income: by naming the payer after an acquirer, adding an invoice reference, or, since D31, paying cash in. The round-trip check, the director-name rule and the indicators surface it, including the three cash indicators, and the line is one click from its figure. Cash split below every limit is the gap that remains, and it is visible only in M18. Since D52, wording that mixes lists cannot reach trading receipts: a payer who adds a second list's word only sends the credit to the queue, and every line-type cue is non-trading, so payer-written cues can lower M2 but never raise it. Since revision 4, a listed name hidden inside a longer word in any alphabet does not match, and since revision 5 that holds after a combining mark too; adding a gateway's name to an invoice-referenced credit does not take it out of the round-trip check. Since revision 6, a typographic dash, a dot or an invisible character can no longer hide a payment-method cue or a lender phrase, and a cheque cannot hide cash wording; a payer can still split a short trading name across words to be read as it (E BAY), which counts as trading receipts as the plain spelling always did, and the irregular-acquirer indicator reads it. Since revision 7, a payer cannot take an ambiguous credit out of the queue by naming a director or an own account in the reference: the name adds a candidate and settles nothing. A payer can still name a director in the reference of a credit the table does not find ambiguous, which moves it to director funding (R5); that lowers M2, never raises it. Third, someone turns the offline lock off and uses a hosted model. The prompt still carries only tables (P5), and the evidence records where data went. Fourth, someone with the underwriter's login edits the case log and its head record together to hide an override; the files cannot show it, but a signed pack pins the head it saw, so the edit shows against every signed pack.\n- **Pseudonymised is not anonymous:** amounts, dates and balances can identify a business again, so the output is labelled as personal data.\n\n## Consumer trace\n\nThese are the shared contracts this change touches, with every consumer found.
