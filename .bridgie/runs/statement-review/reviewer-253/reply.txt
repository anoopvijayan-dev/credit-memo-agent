My verdict on T29 is `fail`: there is one major defect against R36, plus one minor one.

I couldn't run any Python to reproduce either finding: running it was denied, as was writing `evidence.json` to the run directory. Both findings come from reading the code, and the step count below is worked out by hand. The runner's contracts log shows all 47 tests in `tests/test_import_openbanking.py` ran and passed.

**What meets R36:**
- **Booked only:** pending lines are left out and counted, and any other status is refused.
- **GBP only:** a non-GBP line or balance refuses the account, which is what `design.md:1227` asks for.
- **Provider ids:** the bank's `TransactionId` is kept as the line id.
- **Balance:** it is rebuilt from the per-line balances or from `--statement-balance`/`--at`, and `contract.walk` checks every per-line balance, so a broken chain is refused.
- **Paged repeats:** a repeated transaction is kept once when identical and refused when it differs.

**Major: some valid files are refused** (`openbanking.py:316`). When transactions share a booking time, the search for their order tries every arrangement of the lines that have no balance before it tries a balanced line that has to come first. With 8 such lines ahead of that balanced line, it passes the 100,000-step cap after about 109,600 steps. The file is then refused with "the export may be missing transactions", though its balance chain holds. That contradicts the producer's claim that valid groups take about one step per line, and R36, which refuses only when the chain breaks. No test covers a mixed group larger than three lines.

**Minor: the pending count can be too high** (`openbanking.py:394`). If a transaction shows as Pending on one page and Booked on another, it is kept as booked and also counted as left out.
