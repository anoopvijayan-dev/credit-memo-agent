**The design.** A review workspace that runs on the underwriter's own machine. It splits all money in into trading receipts and named buckets: cash deposits, own-account transfers, director and shareholder money, lending drawdowns, refunds and returned payments, HMRC repayments, other explained money in, and unexplained. The buckets must add up to total money in to the penny.

- Every figure and monthly cell links to the lines behind it.
- The unclear lines are listed first.
- The underwriter can correct a line's category with a reason, and each correction is logged.
- They sign off by name, and that writes a pack ready to paste into their credit memo.

Arithmetic stays in code, as ADR 001 requires. The model is off by default behind an offline lock, and there is no pass/refer/fail verdict. The design follows the friend's reply: most of the time goes on judging what each line is, not adding up, and data should stay on the firm's machine.

**Options rejected:**
- **A static HTML report plus an overrides file:** corrections would happen outside the page. It survives as the format of the signed record.
- **A spreadsheet interface:** figures lose their link to the evidence as soon as a cell is edited.
- **Hosted SaaS now:** applicant data would leave the firm.
- **A model categorising the unclear lines:** it would send payment text out and make up the very judgement the underwriter is afraid of.

**Scope.** There are 54 requirements and 18 properties, split into two tiers.
- **Tier 1 (37), for the 2026-11-29 demo:** the buckets, real UK card acquirers and lenders, a monthly summary, risk indicators, the workspace, corrections, the pack and the offline lock.

The workspace listens only on loopback, requires a random per-launch token on every request, rejects any Host header that is not the loopback address (against DNS rebinding) and sends no permissive CORS headers. Writes also need the expected log position.\n- **Trust boundary:** bank data the applicant controls enters through the importers, and the loader checks its structure and balance chain. Descriptions are written by payers: they are escaped in HTML under a strict content security policy with no inline script, formula characters are neutralised in CSV, and they never go into prompts.\n- **Secrets:** an optional model API key in the environment, only when the offline lock is off. It is never logged or written to evidence.\n- **Personal data:** statements, the profile (director names, account identifiers), override reasons and packs stay in the case folder. Evidence holds ids, figures and hashes, not descriptions or the profile. Purge deletes the folder and leaves a ledger line with no personal data. Encryption at rest is the firm's disk encryption; the tool adds none of its own. Retention follows the firm's policy, and purge is how the tool carries it out.\n- **Risk path:** lending data (risk: data). The human acknowledgement is the named sign-off bound to the snapshot. The name is typed, not authenticated (README known limit), so the OS user is recorded alongside it. Real sign-in belongs to a hosted version.\n- **Worst cases with this deployed:** First, an applicant writes script into a payment reference to act in the underwriter's browser, for example to change categories or sign off. Escaping, the content security policy and the token stop it. Second, an applicant makes money look like trading income by naming the payer after an acquirer or adding an invoice reference. The round-trip check, the director-name rule and the indicators surface it, and the line is one click from its figure. Third, someone turns the offline lock off and uses a hosted model. The prompt still carries only tables (P5), and the evidence records where data went.\n- **Pseudonymised is not anonymous:** amounts, dates and balances can identify a business again, so the output is labelled as personal data.\n\n## Consumer trace\n\nThese are the shared contracts this change touches, with every consumer found.
