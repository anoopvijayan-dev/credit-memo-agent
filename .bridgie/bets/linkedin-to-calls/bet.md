# Building credit-memo-agent in public on LinkedIn turns into discovery calls with lenders

## Who

Heads of credit, lending-ops leads and fintech CTOs at UK SME lenders and brokers who scroll LinkedIn and would hire or buy from a senior engineer they have watched build something relevant to them.

## Problem in their words

ASSUMPTION: they don't trust "AI underwriting" vendors because they can't see how figures are produced, and they don't know any engineer who builds agents for regulated lending.

## Why now

LinkedIn distribution now favours topic relevance over network size (van der Blom 2026, 1.3M posts); document carousels get roughly three times the engagement of text; reach for active creators fell ~60% over two years, so consistency on one topic matters more than audience size. Anoop plans to start freelancing in about six months and has no public body of work yet.

## What they do today

Hire from agencies or vendors they already know; follow large AI influencers who don't work in lending.

## Expected outcome

Over 6 weeks (3 posts/week + 1 newsletter, from 2026-11-09): at least 5 DMs or comments from people in the target segment, at least 3 discovery calls booked, at least 1 statement run through the agent by an outside analyst. Read from a weekly tally of DMs, profile views from the segment, and calls. Baseline today: zero.

## Open questions

Raised by the grill.
- D1: Problem in their words is an ASSUMPTION. Before post 1, has any head of credit, lending-ops lead or lender CTO actually said they distrust AI underwriting because they can't trace the figures? Needs interviews (e.g. 5 people from the NACFB list), not memory.
- D2: Is the segment on LinkedIn at all? Evidence task 1 (how many of the 20 outreach targets posted or commented in the last 30 days) hasn't been run. If 3 or fewer, the channel is wrong whatever the algorithm does.
- D3: The 'baseline zero' leaves out the starting audience. The 'interest graph' still needs some first readers in the segment. Export your LinkedIn connections and count those whose title or company matches the 'who' (credit, lending ops, CTO at UK SME lenders or brokers).
- D4: Attribution and readability: the playbook's week-3 outreach to 10 brokers and lenders falls inside the same 6 weeks, so outreach calls will be counted as post-driven. 'Target segment' has no named list, and LinkedIn's free tier doesn't show reliable job titles for profile viewers. How will the tally separate the two?
- D5: Credibility: the 7 Nov panel failed v0.1 (C1 blocker: invented `£48k`/`£1.2m` pass the guard; C3: sign error flips the policy). The bet asks for an outside analyst to run a statement through the agent in this window. What ships before the first post on 9 Nov?
- D6: Buying path: today they hire agencies or vendors they already know, which is tolerable partly because those firms already passed vendor and outsourcing due diligence. Would a UK SME lender take on an individual freelancer outside that? Ask 3 heads of credit how they found and onboarded their last outside tech supplier.
- D7: Gate: evidence task 2 (contract social-media and IP clauses) is unresolved, and the bet's own kill criterion depends on it. A clause on outside work, conflict of interest or competing with the employer could stop public work on a lending product, even with no employer named.
