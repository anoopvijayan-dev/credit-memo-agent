# Small-business underwriters lose most of each application to reading bank statements, and would hand that part to an agent if it never invents a number

## Who

An underwriter at a lender that makes small-business loans. Right now that is one friend of the owner. They work through a queue of applications, and for each one they must read several months of the applicant's business bank statements before they can write the credit memo. Their name goes on the file they sign. (Assumption: lender type, firm size, country and number of underwriters are unknown. The repo's Monzo import suggests the UK, but nobody has confirmed it.)

## Problem in their words

No direct quotes yet. Everything below is the owner paraphrasing one conversation, and none of it is backed by a ticket, an interview note or data.

- "I'm buried in applications." They cancelled a weekend road trip to catch up. (paraphrase, second-hand)
- Most of the time on each application goes on reading months of bank statements line by line before the memo can be written. (paraphrase; the share of time has not been measured)
- "If the AI makes up a number, it's my name on the file." This is given as the reason the firm won't allow generic AI. (assumption: our wording of the firm's stated reason)
- The tools built for this job are being phased out or cost too much. (paraphrase; which tools and what they cost is unknown)

## Why now

The owner reports two triggers. Neither has been checked:

1. The tools the firm could use for statement analysis are being withdrawn or priced out of reach, so the cheaper option is disappearing. (assumption: tools and dates not named)
2. Application volume has grown to the point where the underwriter gives up weekends. (assumption: we don't know since when, or whether it is seasonal)

Better language models are not a trigger from the customer's side. If neither claim survives a check, the honest answer is "nothing changed for them; the backlog is chronic".

## What they do today

They read every statement by hand, line by line. Probably that means PDFs downloaded from the applicant's bank, with figures (income, outgoings, average balance, bounced payments, existing loan repayments) totted up in a spreadsheet or by eye, then typed into the memo. (assumption: the exact format and tooling are unknown)

- **Generic AI:** firm policy forbids it, because nothing traces a figure back to a transaction.
- **Dedicated statement-analysis or Open Banking categorisation products:** they have used or looked at these, but say they are being phased out or cost too much. (assumption: products not named)
- **Alternative:** staying slow. The cost of the backlog falls on the underwriter's own time, not on the firm's budget.

## Expected outcome

**Metric:** hours this underwriter spends reading statements per application.

- **Baseline:** unknown. All we have is "most of each application". We need a timed baseline on 3 to 5 real files, read the usual way.
- **Target:** at least half the time cut on the same number of comparable files where the agent does the statement reading.
- **Trust condition:** every figure in the draft must link to the transactions behind it, with zero figures that can't be traced. The underwriter must say they would sign a file that uses the agent's figures, after checking them.
- **When it can be read:** by 2026-11-29, the end of the 2-week appetite. That date only holds if we can get anonymised or real statements in time; otherwise we can't read the result at all.

- D53: How to read D52 in R4. The amended R4 sends a credit to the judgement queue when its line-type cues disagree, or, with no cue, when its name lists point to two buckets. - D54: What settles an ambiguous credit (amended R4 against R5, R6 and R8). R5, R6 and R8 say a credit from a director or own account named in the profile, or a paired own-account transfer, SHALL take that category. To keep them as written, the amended R4 lets a profile name or a pairing settle an ambiguous credit, as well as a per-line override; a firm rule never does. All three give non-trading buckets, so nothing ambiguous reaches trading receipts without a person. The alternative keeps every ambiguous credit in the queue until a per-line override, which means amending R5, R6 and R8.
- D61: Category inside one bucket (design C1 step 6, revision 4). Two rules can agree on the bucket but carry different categories: FPS CREDIT STRIPE INV 17 matches a gateway (card settlement) and an invoice reference (customer transfer); RETURNED DD REFUND, with no bank code at the start, matches a returned-payment cue and a refund cue. Today rule order picks, and the fifth T03 review showed that reordering rules can move a credit out of M2, because the round-trip check reads only customer transfers. M7, M15 and the M16 numerator also read these categories. The design fixes an order in the table that puts first the category with more checks. The cost: such a gateway payout counts as a named customer in M15, and such a refund counts in M7 as a returned direct debit.
- D66: Case folders on a network share (R42 scope, D65). The owner's scope says case folders must be on a local disk, because on a network share the operating system may emulate the lock per process, and then two CaseLog objects in one process could both write. - D67: How to read 'a reader never excuses a torn or tampered log as another writer's append' (D65; R43, P15). A writer adds its entry and then records it as complete. A reader that looks in between, while that writer still holds the case, sees an incomplete last entry and cannot tell it from one a crash left. The design shows the recorded entries and an append in progress only in that moment and never shows the entry as complete. Anything wrong at or before the recorded entries is reported whoever holds the case. A lock held by the reader's own thread through another handle or path, or a lock error, does not count as another writer (D50, verify-34). Sign-off decides while holding the case, so it always sees a torn entry. This is what T12 builds. Read literally, 'never' would make every reader report an incomplete entry even while another window's save is landing.
- D75: cma case check while a case is being saved (R43, revision 6). R43 now says a reader reports what follows the recorded entries as being saved, never as intact. cma case check exits 0 for an intact log and 3 for a broken or incomplete one. The design gives being saved its own exit, 4, whether the reader judged the recorded entries or could check nothing, so that a script or the trial sheet never reads a log being saved as intact; the cost is that a check run while the workspace saves exits non-zero and should be repeated. The T12 worktree today exits 4 only when it could check nothing, and exits 0 with a note when the recorded entries check out.
- D76: Cheque wording in R4's text (D70). D70 made cheque wording a payment-method cue with no bucket, so a cheque with cash paid-in wording is ambiguous. R4 says a credit is ambiguous 'when a payment rail appears with cash paid-in wording', and the line-type cue term lists the rail as Faster Payment, BACS, CHAPS, a transfer and a standing order. D74 asked for no other requirement change, so revision 6 leaves R4 as it is, and the design reads a cheque as a payment rail, since cheques clear through the cheque clearing system. T03's reviewer judges R4 by its own text. Read narrowly, CHQ CASH DEPOSIT would be a cash deposit under R4, but the build makes it ambiguous.
- D81: R5 and R6 against a protected verdict (revision 7). R5 says each credit whose counterparty matches a director named in the profile goes to director funding; R6 says the same for own accounts. Since revision 3 the design's precedence, and T03's build (tests/test_rules_v2.py:335-346 in the T03 worktree), let a protected verdict decide first: a line-type cue (returned payment, own-pot move, HMRC, refund) or lender, generic loan, director, capital-introduced or shareholder wording. So REFUND JANE DOE or JANE DOE LOAN, with Jane Doe a director, is a refund or a lending drawdown, not director funding. D79 said R5 and R6 still decide every credit the table does not find ambiguous, and limited this revision's changes to them, so revision 7 does not add the exception. T05, which builds R5 and R6, will be judged on their text.
- D83: R6 and debits (revision 8). D82 asks R5 and R6 to say that a protected verdict decides a credit before a case-profile name does. R6 covers lines in either direction, and T03 reads the protected rules before an own account's name on a debit as well (src/cma/categorise.py:580-585 in the T03 worktree; tests/test_rules_v2.py:438-449 asserts that UNPAID DD followed by an own account's name is a returned direct debit in both directions). - D90: Candidates of a credit that only the two profile kinds make ambiguous (R5, R6, revision 9). The requirements' candidates term is every rule the credit matched in either stage, then each kind of profile name. The owner's D86 change in the T03 worktree records, for a credit the table finds unambiguous, only the rule that would have decided it, and no table candidate when only a payment-method cue matched (src/cma/categorise.py:628 at 79965d4). Revision 9 follows the term, so the queue shows every reading. The cost is a one-line change in T03 and longer candidate lists.
- D91: Where R5 and R6 name the judgement queue (D88). D88 says such a credit goes to unexplained money in and the judgement queue. Revision 9 has R5 and R6 mark it, place it in unexplained money in and record its candidates, then say the queue lists it under R15. R15 already lists every unexplained line and shows each ambiguous credit's candidates with their buckets. This follows D58 and D60, which moved R4's queue clause to R15 so that T03 could complete R4. - D101: R4: the bank's code against a longer listed phrase (verify-53, first major). R4 says a returned-payment code the bank prints at the start of the line (UNPAID DD) settles the line type on its own. It also says a match inside a longer listed phrase counts only as the longer phrase. The shipped table never makes the two meet, but a table edit can. With UNPAID DD TO SAVINGS on the own-pot list, T03 at d507393 counts the bank's code as inside the longer phrase, and the credit becomes an own-account transfer. The reviewer reads R4 as giving the code the line type. The design (C1 step 2) lets the bank's code settle the line type before the longer-phrase rule applies, so the credit is a returned payment. Both readings are non-trading, so M2 does not move; M7 and the returned-DD indicator do.
- D102: R4: alternatives inside one pattern (verify-53, second major). Since D97 a rule's pattern and its names are scanned separately and the longest match at each start is kept. Inside one pattern, though, Python's engine returns the first alternative that matches at a start. With ACME and ACME FASTER PAYMENT LTD written as two alternatives of one pattern, the shorter one wins. The rail inside the longer phrase then survives, and ACME FASTER PAYMENT LTD CASH DEPOSIT becomes ambiguous instead of trading receipts. The design (C1 step 1) takes the longest match any alternative can make at a start, so the order of alternatives picks nothing, just as rule order picks nothing. The standard engine does not do this: the build needs a longest-match engine, or a matcher its tests prove equivalent, and must still meet the 2-second target at 10,000 lines (R52). No shipped pattern has two alternatives that start at the same place.
- D103: A repair under lock-file interference (R42 and P15 scope, D99). D99 says that if removing or replacing a lock file still lets two writers append, the log they leave is reported broken, never intact. That holds for appends: two entries claim one position, so the chain or the head record fails there. Dropping a damaged (torn) entry is not an append. It rewrites the log through a temporary file and a rename, so that a crash leaves either the old log or the repaired one. Suppose interference lets a second writer take the case, drop the same torn entry and append, all between the first writer's re-check and its rename. The rename then removes the second writer's entries, the first writer's head record matches what is left, and the log reads intact. The design states this as part of the scope, like a hand edit of the log and its head record together, which only a signed pack's pinned head shows.
- D108: R42's 're-check before each write' (verify-54, verify-55; revision 10 scope). An append changes the case's files in up to three steps: the empty head record before the first line, the line, and the rename that publishes the new head record. A repair renames a rewritten log over the old one, then renames the head record. Each rename is prepared in a temporary file that nobody reads. Revision 10's design said a writer re-checks its lock before each append and each repair. T12 at 6e73302 re-checks before the first step and before writing a head record's temporary file. verify-55 reads R42 as asking for a re-check before every step that changes a file the case holds. It failed T12 on two majors: there is no re-check between the empty head record and the line, and none between a temporary file and the rename that publishes it. The design now reads R42 as the reviewer does. A re-check never closes the window between itself and its step; revision 10 already states that window. A step refused after the line leaves an unrecorded entry, as a crash does, and the next holder drops it.
- D109: What a reader reports when one writer waits at the gate while another holds the case (R43, P15; verify-54, verify-55). A check that finds the gate busy tries the case lock directly (D98). If the case is held too, the check does not hold the gate, so it cannot know that one writer held the case through its read. The holder can let go, leaving an incomplete entry, and the writer at the gate can take the case between the check's two looks. This is verify-47's corner, which the gate closes only for checks that hold it. Since D105, T12 names a fault at or before the recorded entries from a steady read, and otherwise claims nothing. verify-55 reads R43 as asking for the recorded entries and the rest as being saved whenever a steady read exists, and failed T12 for claiming nothing. Reporting being saved there would let a check say being saved over an incomplete entry left during a free moment in its read. R43 says a reader of a free case names that entry.
- D110: A v2 pattern that can match across words (R4 after D102; table validation). Since D102, a rule's match is the longest its pattern can make where it starts, and R4 counts a match inside a longer one only as the longer one. A table edit could add a pattern with a repeat that can match across a space, such as .* or [A-Z ]+. That pattern would take in every other list's wording after its start: SQUARE.* on the card list would make SQUARE UK CASH ADVANCE a card settlement in trading receipts instead of ambiguous. No shipped pattern has such a repeat. Of the ten patterns in v2, only the invoice reference repeats without limit, and only whitespace and digits. verify-53's two majors were table edits, so T03's next review may try this one.
- D125: R42 (revision 13, D124) says that under lock-file interference, a log that reads intact holds every save whose writer was told it was recorded. At b428d56 a save is told it was recorded as soon as its head record's rename returns, and a repair re-checks only its lock before its renames. In this schedule the guarantee fails. A's re-check before its head rename passes. The lock file is replaced, and B takes the new lock, drops A's line as torn and renames the repaired log. A's head record lands, and A is told its save was recorded. Then B's head record lands over A's. The log reads intact without A's save. The design default closes this with two checks. A save is told it was recorded only after it reads back that the log holds its entry where its head record lists it. A repair re-checks, before each rename, that the log and the head record are still as its check read them. A repair refused after its log rename leaves a broken log, never an intact one. The alternative keeps the build as it is and writes the exception into R42.
- D126: verify-65's first major finding against R4. v2 accepts an unless or a requires on every rule except the bank-code rule. A table edit that adds unless: FPS to the cash rule makes FPS CASH DEPOSIT STRIPE a card settlement in trading receipts instead of ambiguous. No shipped v2 rule has had an exception since D70, and the design's no-hidden-tie-breaks rule says none should. D49 allowed an exception only if it excludes wording that is on no list, but no validator can check that against every other pattern (the D101 argument). The owner's 1b592b6 does not change this.
- D127: The owner's 1b592b6 never counts cash paid-in wording as inside a payment-method cue's longer phrase. So with FPS CASH DEPOSIT on the rail list, FPS CASH DEPOSIT is ambiguous (rail and cash), not a rail alone. That is a second exception to R4's longer-phrase clause, beside the bank's code (D101), and R4's text does not state it. T03's reviewer judges R4 by its own text. The longer-phrase clause and the rail-with-cash clause meet here as they did in D101.
- D132: A payment-method cue inside a longer cash phrase (R4; revision 14, after 4adddf9). D127 keeps cash paid-in wording from being taken in by a payment-method cue's longer phrase, so FPS CASH DEPOSIT listed on the rail list is ambiguous. The other side is still open. A table edit can list FPS CASH DEPOSIT, or CHEQUES AND CASH PAID IN, on the cash list. At 4adddf9 (src/cma/categorise.py:762-765) the rail or cheque inside that phrase then counts only as the cash phrase. FPS CASH DEPOSIT becomes a cash deposit in trading receipts and M18, though the line says the money came by Faster Payment. R4's longer-phrase clause allows that reading, and its rail-with-cash clause forbids it. T03's reviewer judges R4 by its own text, and verify-53 to verify-67's majors were all table edits. No shipped cash phrase holds payment-method wording, so no shipped verdict moves either way.

## Idea

An underwriter friend doing small-business loans cancelled a weekend road trip because he is buried in applications. Most of the time goes on reading months of bank statements line by line before the memo can be written. His firm will not let generic AI near it because a made-up number lands on the person who signs the file, and the tools built for this job are being phased out or cost too much. We want to test whether an agent that computes every figure in code, cites each one to the bank transactions behind it, and leaves the decision to a named human would be trusted to take the statement reading off them.
