Hey, no worries about the trip at all!

Happy to help. I'll give you a rough idea of how things work from an underwriting perspective.

1. Time spent reviewing bank statements

It depends on the business and how many accounts they operate.

For a straightforward application with 6–12 months of statements, I'd estimate around 45–60 minutes. For more complicated businesses with multiple accounts, it can easily take 90 minutes or more.

Roughly:

* 10–20 minutes calculating monthly credits, debits, average balances and existing repayments.
* 25–45 minutes understanding unusual transactions, internal transfers, director loans, cash deposits and other unclear payments.
* Another 10–20 minutes reconciling everything and preparing the credit summary.

The basic calculations aren't the hardest part. It's identifying what is genuine trading income and what isn't.

For example, a business might receive £30k in a month, but £10k could be a director's loan and £5k a transfer from another account. We shouldn't assess affordability based on the full £30k.

2. How do statements come in?

We get a mixture.

If I take 15 applications as an example, perhaps 9 would involve PDF statements, 4 would come through Open Banking and 2 would be a combination.

Open Banking is easier when customers successfully connect their accounts, but PDFs are still common, especially with smaller businesses.

3. Open Banking provider and data

Providers such as TrueLayer, Yapily or Plaid are commonly encountered in the UK market.

Typically, we can access transaction dates, descriptions, amounts and balances. Depending on the service and integration, we might also get categorisation and cash-flow summaries.

Even with categorised data, I wouldn't blindly trust everything.

For example, payments through Stripe, SumUp or other merchant processors need reviewing. Likewise, transfers between business accounts can inflate turnover if counted twice.

I'd need to confirm the exact provider and what our current integration offers.

4. Lender or broker? Who approves?

Assuming we're a direct lender, the underwriting team assesses applications and makes credit recommendations.

We review bank statements, credit bureau reports, Companies House records, financial accounts, existing borrowing and repayment capacity.

Smaller loans might be approved within delegated authority, while larger or higher-risk cases go to a senior credit manager or credit committee.

We don't simply approve based on bank turnover.

5. Credit memo template

A typical Credit Appraisal Memo would include:

* Business details and Companies House number
* Loan amount, purpose and term
* Business trading history
* Monthly turnover and bank credits
* Average and minimum account balances
* Existing loans, overdrafts and other commitments
* Returned direct debits and payment issues
* VAT/HMRC payments and arrears indicators
* Director loans and intercompany transfers
* Credit history, CCJs and defaults
* Cash-flow affordability and debt service
* Key risks and mitigants
* Underwriter recommendation

If your tool could automatically populate the financial sections, that would definitely help.

6. Have applications increased?

I'd say we've experienced some busier periods, but I wouldn't put a percentage on it without checking our application volumes.

The issue isn't just volume. A complicated case can take much longer than three straightforward applications, and that creates a backlog.

7. What would make the tool worth using?

For me, accuracy is more important than just saving time.

I'd want to click any number in the report and see exactly which transactions make up that figure.

I'd particularly want it to:

* Identify genuine business revenue and exclude internal transfers.
* Highlight director loans, unusual credits and large cash deposits.
* Recognise existing loan repayments and merchant cash advances.
* Flag returned direct debits, persistent overdraft usage and other risk indicators.
* Allow me to manually correct categories.
* Produce a summary I can use directly in my credit memo.

If it reduced a 60-minute review to 25–30 minutes while maintaining accuracy, that would be a strong selling point.

But I wouldn't want AI making the final lending decision. I'd want it assisting the underwriter.

8. What might prevent a pilot?

Data protection and accuracy would probably be the biggest concerns.

We're dealing with sensitive business and sometimes personal financial information.

The firm would need confidence that data is secure, access is controlled and there's an audit trail.

A tool that operates entirely on our own machine or within our own environment, without transmitting customer data externally, could be attractive.

But the IT/compliance team would still need to approve it.

I'd start with synthetic or properly anonymised sample statements and compare the results with our existing process before introducing it into live underwriting.

9. Have we looked at other tools?

There are established providers offering bank statement analysis and automated underwriting support, including LendingMetrics, AccountScore and other specialist vendors.

I couldn't give you a reliable quote without checking what was evaluated internally.

For a small lender, the concern is often the overall commercial commitment, rather than just the cost of analysing one statement.

We wouldn't necessarily need another large underwriting platform if all we're trying to solve is bank-statement review.

I'd be more interested in something affordable, easy to trial, and transparent enough to verify every calculation.

One additional thought

The biggest challenge isn't extracting transactions from statements. Plenty of existing tools already do that.

It's interpreting the transactions correctly.

If a tool could reliably tell me that £80k entered the business account but only £55k represented genuine trading receipts, explain the remaining £25k, and let me verify everything, that would be genuinely useful.

That's where I think the value is for smaller lending teams.
