Revision 17, after D148 (changes requested under the owner's standing approval of 2026-11-19, after T12's twenty-fourth review, verify-72, and T03's twenty-third, verify-73; the owner had answered revision 16's questions as D142 to D144). R4 now says that a match set aside inside a longer listed phrase sets nothing aside even when its rule counts by another match elsewhere in the line. Matches are settled from the longest down, so whether a match counts rests only on the longer matches that hold it, and a rule counts, and is among the candidates, where any match of its own counts (D142). With FPS CASH DEPOSIT STRIPE listed as a cash paid-in phrase and FPS CASH DEPOSIT and STRIPE as a gateway's names, FPS CASH DEPOSIT STRIPE | STRIPE is ambiguous, with the rail, the cash paid-in rule and the gateway as its candidates, not a cash deposit (verify-73). In this schedule it fails. A save passes its re-check before its head rename, and the case lock file is replaced. A repair takes the new lock, finds the save's line torn and, once a person confirms it, renames the repaired log and passes its re-check before its head rename. The save's head record lands, its read-back finds its entry gone, and the take-back finds the head record its own. The repair's head record lands, the repair reads back an intact log and is told recorded, and the take-back's rename puts back the older head record. The repair's entry is then past the recorded entries as a torn write, and once a person confirms dropping it, the log reads intact without a save told recorded. A take-back that re-checks the lock files first, as every head rename does, finds the replaced case lock and is not made; the save is then not confirmed, which also answers verify-72's third finding. R42 therefore keeps the take-back's rename among the steps a writer re-checks both case lock files before, and revision 17 asks the owner. Every other requirement, property, term and id is as revision 16 left it.

Terms. *The system*: the cma package running on the underwriter's machine. *Figures layer*: src/cma/metrics.py and a new indicators module, the only code that does arithmetic on money (ADR 001). *Case*: one application, holding one or more of the applicant's accounts, a profile, an override log and a case log. *The reply*: the underwriter's written answers of 2026-11-16 (one anecdotal source; see evidence-synthesis.md). *Judgement queue*: the lines the workspace lists first because a person has to decide what they are. *Policy check*: the pass, refer or fail outcome and the rule results from the packaged policy (src/cma/policies/uk_sme_default.yaml). Its rule ids (R1 to R9, cited as [R1] to [R9] in a pack) are not the requirement ids in this file. *Illustrative label*: the sentence shown with every policy outcome, saying that the thresholds ship with the tool, are not the firm's lending policy, and do not make the lending decision. *Preview*: the pack's content and its snapshot hash, shown before sign-off; nothing is written until sign-off. *Line-type cue* (revision 3; widened in revision 7): wording that says what kind of line it is: a returned payment, a move to or from the applicant's own pot or savings, HMRC, a refund or reversal, or a payment-method cue. *Payment-method cue* (revision 7): wording that says how the money came when it did not come as cash: the payment rail (Faster Payment, BACS, CHAPS, a transfer, a standing order) or cheque wording. It is a line-type cue that belongs to no bucket. *Name list* (revision 3): a maintained list of who sent a credit or what it is for: acquirers, gateways, marketplaces, delivery platforms, lenders and merchant cash advance providers, director and capital-introduced wording, cash paid-in wording, invoice references and explained non-trading words. *Candidates* (revision 4; widened in revision 7): every rule an ambiguous credit matched in either stage, that is among its line-type cues and among the name lists, counting a match inside a longer listed phrase only as the longer phrase; and each kind of case-profile name its wording matches, a director or shareholder (R5) and an own account (R6), each giving its own bucket. A payment-method cue can be a candidate; it gives no bucket. *Protected verdict* (revision 8): the category the rule table gives a line whose wording it does not find ambiguous, when the rule that decides is protected: a line-type cue other than a payment-method cue (a returned payment, a move to or from the applicant's own pot or savings, HMRC, a refund or reversal), or the wording of a lender or merchant cash advance provider, a generic loan or advance, a director, owner or shareholder, or capital introduced, and on a debit owner drawings or dividend wording. These are R21's patterns and the line-type cues. A payment-method cue alone gives no verdict, and every protected verdict is non-trading. *Case-log writer* (revision 5): anything that appends to a case's log: a workspace window, a cma command, a thread, or a separate CaseLog object (a handle on the log) in the same process. A case is its folder: two paths to the same folder, a symlink among them, lead to the same log and the same lock. *Recorded entries* (revision 6): the entries the case log's head record, a file kept beside the log, lists as complete. A writer adds its entry to the log and then records it in the head record, so for a moment the log can hold more than the recorded entries. *Being saved* (revision 6): what a reader reports while another case-log writer holds the case: the recorded entries it judged, and that what follows them is not yet checked. It is neither intact nor broken. It replaces revision 5's append in progress. *Case lock files* (revision 10): the two files the tool keeps beside a case's log to order its writers: the gate, which a writer passes alone and briefly to take the case and which a reader holds while it reads, so that no writer takes the case during a read; and the case lock, which the writer holding the case holds. Neither holds data. *Steady read* (revision 12): a read of the case log across which the head record did not change. *Save* (revision 13): one append or one repair by a case-log writer, from its check of the log to the head record that lists its entry; the writer then tells its caller whether the save was recorded. *Repair* (revision 13): dropping an entry past the recorded entries, the torn write that a crash or a refused save left, once a person has confirmed it; the repair records who confirmed it in an entry of its own, so it is a save. *Not confirmed* (revision 17; widened in revision 18): what a save's writer is told when the save's head record may list its entry but the save could not confirm it: the save's read-back failed and its head record was not then taken back, or the rename that published its head record was made and the head record could not then be made durable. It is neither that the save was recorded nor that it was not. The case log, once checked, shows whether the save's entry is among the recorded entries.

## R4 · ubiquitous · risk: money
THE SYSTEM SHALL recognise, from maintained lists shipped as data: credits from UK card acquirers, payment gateways, marketplaces and delivery platforms as trading receipts; lines naming UK business lenders and merchant cash advance providers as lending drawdowns (money in) or debt service (money out); moves between the applicant's own pots or savings as own-account transfers; HMRC credits as HMRC repayments; refund and reversal credits as refunds; and cash paid in at a counter or machine as cash deposits.
THE SYSTEM SHALL treat a credit's wording as ambiguous when its line-type cues point to more than one money-in bucket, when a payment-method cue (a payment rail or cheque wording) appears with cash paid-in wording, or, when no line-type cue points to a money-in bucket, when the name lists it matches point to more than one money-in bucket; SHALL mark an ambiguous credit as ambiguous, place it in unexplained money in, and record as its candidates every rule it matched in either stage and each kind of case-profile name its wording matches; and SHALL NOT count it as trading receipts unless the underwriter moves it there with an override (R17). Only a per-line override (R17) or an own-account pairing (R8) settles an ambiguous credit; a firm rule does not. A case-profile name (R5, R6) that the credit's wording also matches adds its bucket as one more candidate and does not settle it, because the description cannot reliably tell the payer from a payment reference that names someone else. Line-type cues that agree outrank name lists that point elsewhere (PAYPAL REFUND is a refund). A match inside a longer listed phrase counts only as the longer phrase (STRIPE CAPITAL is a lending drawdown, not also a card settlement; HMRC REFUND is an HMRC repayment), and only a match that itself counts can set another aside inside its longer phrase: a match set aside inside a longer listed phrase sets nothing aside, even when its rule counts by another match elsewhere in the line. Matches are settled from the longest down, so whether a match counts rests only on the longer matches that hold it, and a rule counts, and is among the candidates, where any match of its own counts (with FPS CASH DEPOSIT STRIPE listed as a cash paid-in phrase and FPS CASH DEPOSIT as a gateway's name, the gateway's match is set aside inside the cash phrase and sets nothing aside, and the cash phrase does not take in the rail, so the rail counts and FPS CASH DEPOSIT STRIPE is ambiguous, not a cash deposit; with STRIPE also a gateway's name, FPS CASH DEPOSIT STRIPE | STRIPE is ambiguous too, though the trailing STRIPE keeps the gateway counting, and its candidates are the rail, the cash paid-in rule and the gateway). A match's length is the longest that any alternative of the rule's pattern or names can make where it starts, so the order of alternatives inside a rule picks nothing (with ACME and ACME FASTER PAYMENT LTD as two alternatives of one rule, ACME FASTER PAYMENT LTD matches whole, and the rail inside it does not count). A returned-payment code the bank prints at the start of the line (UNPAID DD) settles the line type on its own, before the longer-phrase rule applies: it never counts as inside another rule's longer listed phrase (with UNPAID DD TO SAVINGS on an own-pot list, UNPAID DD TO SAVINGS is still a returned payment). Cash paid-in wording and a payment-method cue likewise never take each other in: neither counts as inside a longer listed phrase that holds the other, whichever list holds that phrase, so a payment-method cue with cash paid-in wording is always ambiguous (with FPS CASH DEPOSIT listed as a payment-method phrase, FPS CASH DEPOSIT is ambiguous, not a payment-method cue alone; with FPS CASH DEPOSIT listed as a cash paid-in phrase, it is ambiguous, not a cash deposit).
No rule of the maintained lists carries an exception: an unless (the rule does not match where other wording also appears), a requires (it matches only where other wording also appears), or an exception of the rule's own pattern: a lookahead or lookbehind (the pattern tests wording beside its match without matching it), or an anchor, ^, $, \A or \Z (the pattern tests whether anything stands before or after its match in the line). Only the pattern of the bank's returned-payment code (UNPAID DD), which the system lists in its own code, keeps an anchor, its ^ at the start of the line, where the bank prints the code. THE SYSTEM SHALL refuse to load a rule table other than the frozen v1 table (R54) in which any rule carries one, that anchor apart, and SHALL name the rule, because an exception could hide one list's wording from the ambiguity check (with an unless naming FPS on the cash list, FPS CASH DEPOSIT STRIPE would be a card settlement; with the cash rule's pattern written KIOSK CASH DEPOSIT(?! STRIPE), so would FPS KIOSK CASH DEPOSIT STRIPE; and with it written CASH DEPOSIT$, so would FPS CASH DEPOSIT STRIPE). Only the frozen v1 table, as its bytes were verified, is spared this check. The table the system loads as v1 (R54) is that table only if its bytes match the hash the system pins in its own code, and THE SYSTEM SHALL compare them before it checks anything else in the table. With any other bytes, whatever the table says of itself, THE SYSTEM SHALL refuse it as a changed frozen table, naming the table, SHALL name after that whatever else keeps it from loading, and SHALL use none of its rules (with v1's rules list emptied, the refusal says first that v1 has been changed, and then that it has no rules). It SHALL do so whatever the change makes fail, a value the format does not allow included: no failure in loading or checking a changed v1 SHALL reach the caller as itself or be named before the change (with frozen: false set on a copy of v1, and a rule given bank_code: true and a pattern given as a list, the refusal says first that v1 has been changed, and then names that rule's pattern among what else keeps it from loading). A changed v1 is not spared the exception check: among what else keeps it from loading, THE SYSTEM SHALL name each of its rules that carries an exception, naming the rule (with unless: FPS added to a rule of a copy of v1 that keeps frozen: true, the refusal says first that v1 has been changed, and then names that rule's unless). Every table the system does not load as v1, whether or not it calls itself frozen or bears v1's id, is checked rule by rule as the maintained lists are, and THE SYSTEM SHALL refuse it, naming the rule, where a rule carries an exception (with frozen: true set on a copy of the v2 table whose cash rule's pattern is CASH DEPOSIT$, the refusal names the cash rule). A value that the table format does not allow in a rule's field, such as a pattern given as a list, a mapping or a number, is a fault of that rule, whatever else the rule carries, the bank-code flag included, and wherever the table's checks read the value: THE SYSTEM SHALL refuse the table naming the rule and the field, with the table's other faults, and SHALL NOT raise it as a type error (with the cash rule's pattern given as the list [CASH DEPOSIT] and unless: FPS on the same rule, the refusal names both of the cash rule's faults; with the returned-payment rule's pattern given as the list [UNPAID DD] beside its bank_code: true, and unless: FPS on the cash rule, the refusal names, among the table's faults, the returned-payment rule's pattern and the cash rule's unless). Any part of the save's line already written stays past the recorded entries, as a crash leaves it. Once any part of a save's line is in the log, which for a repair is once its repaired log has been renamed over the log, no part of what the save's writer is told, the words of any refusal or error it carries included, SHALL say that nothing was written. That holds whatever the save's answer and whatever stopped it: a refusal under the scope below, a failure of the file system, or a take-back that is not made. Each report gives one answer, and its causes, the words of any refusal or error it carries or is raised from, give no other. No part of a not-recorded report SHALL say that the save was recorded or that it is not confirmed. No part of a not-confirmed report SHALL say that the save was recorded, that it was not recorded, or that nothing was written; where its take-back was not made, it SHALL name what stopped the take-back in the save's own words, as the head record no longer the save's own, a case lock file removed or replaced, or the operating-system error it met, named, and SHALL NOT quote the take-back's refusal.

## R43 · unwanted · risk: data
IF the case log's hash chain is broken or its last entry is incomplete, THEN THE SYSTEM SHALL NOT accept a sign-off, and SHALL name the first bad entry. A sign-off decides as a writer holding the case, so it judges the whole log, and an incomplete last entry never passes for a complete one.
IF a reader holds the case, or finds it free, THEN THE SYSTEM SHALL report an entry changed, removed or reordered as broken and an incomplete last entry as incomplete, naming the entry.
WHILE another case-log writer holds the case, a reader SHALL judge only the recorded entries, from a steady read; SHALL report a change, removal or reordering at or before them as broken, naming the entry; and SHALL report anything after them as being saved (the recorded entries, and the rest not yet checked), never as intact and never as broken. A steady read SHALL give the recorded entries, and the rest as being saved, only when one writer held the case through it, which the reader knows when it held the gate through the read, so that no other writer could take the case. A reader that found the case lock free and held it shared knows that no writer could take the case during its read, and judges the whole log as a reader that finds the case free.
A reader's read SHALL count only while the lock files it counts on are still the case's lock files. A reader that holds the case, sign-off included, counts on the case lock it holds and the gate it passed to take the case; a reader that finds the gate busy and holds the case lock shared counts on that case lock; and any other reader counts on the gate it holds through the read and on the case lock behind it, whether it holds that case lock shared or finds it held. IF one of them was removed or replaced during the read, THEN the reader SHALL take the read again, as a reader that does not hold the case if it held the case, and SHALL claim nothing from the read across it; IF that happens on every attempt within its retries, THEN the reader SHALL claim nothing about any entry. A read taken again SHALL go through the gate and the case lock, as the reader's first attempt did. A lock file the reader counts on that is missing when the reader compares it after its read was removed during the read; it says nothing about whether a writer has started on the case, so the reader SHALL NOT then read as a reader that finds no gate. A reader that finds the gate busy, holds the case lock shared and finds that case lock removed during its read therefore looks again through the gate and the case lock.
Scope (revision 17): beyond a read across a lock file the reader counts on, lock-file interference is outside what a reader can know. A lock file removed or replaced before the reader's read leaves the lock files the reader counts on the case's, while a writer that passed the old gate can still take the case through it, so the reader may report being saved over an entry another writer left incomplete, or report a live append as incomplete. A check made after the interference stops gives the true answer.

IF, within its retries, the reader gets no steady read, or every attempt finds the gate busy and the case lock held with no such fault in a steady read, THEN the reader SHALL report the case as being saved and SHALL claim nothing about any entry.
A lock that the reader's own thread holds through another CaseLog object or another path to the case folder, and a lock that cannot be taken or tested, are not another holder.
