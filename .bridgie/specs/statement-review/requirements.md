It replaces revision 5's append in progress. *Case lock files* (revision 10): the two files the tool keeps beside a case's log to order its writers: the gate, which a writer passes alone and briefly to take the case and which a reader holds while it reads, so that no writer takes the case during a read; and the case lock, which the writer holding the case holds. Neither holds data. *Steady read* (revision 12): a read of the case log across which the head record did not change. *Save* (revision 13): one append or one repair by a case-log writer, from its check of the log to the head record that lists its entry; the writer then tells its caller whether the save was recorded. *Repair* (revision 13): dropping an entry past the recorded entries, the torn write that a crash or a refused save left, once a person has confirmed it; the repair records who confirmed it in an entry of its own, so it is a save. *Not confirmed* (revision 17; widened in revision 18): what a save's writer is told when the save's head record may list its entry but the save could not confirm it: the save's read-back failed and its head record was not then taken back, or the rename that published its head record was made and the head record could not then be made durable. It is neither that the save was recorded nor that it was not. ## R2 · ubiquitous · risk: money
THE SYSTEM SHALL compute every money figure, bucket, monthly cell, indicator comparison and comparison difference in integer pence from GBP amounts, in the figures layer only, and never in the review workspace, the pack renderer or a model.
THE SYSTEM SHALL read every amount and balance exactly from the statement's text and count it in whole pence, so that no penny of a statement it accepts is lost before it is counted. IF a line's amount or balance is £10,000,000,000,000 or more either way (a credit or a debit, in credit or overdrawn), or a statement's money in or money out totals £10,000,000,000,000 or more, THEN THE SYSTEM SHALL refuse the statement when it is imported or loaded, naming the line, or saying that its money in or money out reaches that amount, and SHALL compute nothing from it, because no figure could then be reported to the penny as the floating-point number the figures are handed on in, which carries fifteen significant digits. ## R43 · unwanted · risk: data

Scope (revision 28): a review left pending by an earlier release resumes under cma resume and is decided, finalised and recorded on the figures this release computes from the review's stored lines (R1, R2, P2), never on the figures the earlier release stored. Its stored memo is checked against those figures by the grounding guard, as any draft is. Where the two sets of figures are the same, the review resumes as before. A policy rule whose figure is the same keeps its stored outcome. Where a figure a policy rule reads differs, THE SYSTEM SHALL take that rule's outcome from the stored policy result when the stored result settles it. The stored result settles it when it holds the rule's comparison and limits and the figure's new value passes the rule, with or without a referral, or fails a rule its stored value already failed. Otherwise THE SYSTEM SHALL take the outcome from the policy file the review was drafted with, and SHALL use that file only when its hash is the hash stored with the review. That file is the policy the stored result came from, so it gives the same outcome for every rule the stored result settles, and once THE SYSTEM uses it, it may take every rule's outcome from it. IF neither settles a rule's outcome, because the stored result does not and the policy file is missing, cannot be read or has another hash, or the review stores no hash, THEN THE SYSTEM SHALL refuse to resume the review, naming at least one figure whose rule neither settles and saying to run cma run on the statement again; SHALL leave the stored review as it was; and SHALL record nothing. Such a refusal keeps this requirement: resuming a review whose policy outcome cannot be known is outside its guarantee, and storing a copy of every policy in the checkpoint is outside this release.
Scope (revision 29): THE SYSTEM SHALL serialise, across the `cma` processes that share a checkpoint file and with a lock file beside it, three steps: starting a review, from the check that its review id is unused to the point where the review waits for the reviewer; refreshing a resumed review, which writes this release's figures into it only while it is still the pending review that was read; and applying a decision, only to a review that is still pending. So two runs never both start one review id, and a review is decided once (the single-decision guarantee): a decision made after another is not recorded and says who decided first, and a decided review is never reopened. Another program writing to the checkpoint file, removing, renaming or replacing the lock file, and a process stopped part-way through a write are outside this guarantee, as they are for a case's files (R42, R43). WHEN a decided review is written to an output path, THE SYSTEM SHALL make the memo and its evidence in full before writing either, and SHALL write each beside its target and move it into place, the evidence first, so that a failure in making them writes neither and no memo is left in place without its evidence. A disk failure, a full disk or an interruption after the decision is recorded and before both files are in place, and another program taking an output path while the review waits, after the paths were checked as free, are outside this guarantee; the decision stays recorded in the checkpoint, and writing a decided review out a second time is outside this release. Under such interference or failure THE SYSTEM reports as it can.
Scope (revision 30): with the offline lock off, THE SYSTEM SHALL keep handing out the MCP server's ASGI apps as the previous release did: sse_app() and streamable_http_app() SHALL each return an ASGI app that an embedder can mount and serve, with the same options, and reading any attribute of it reads the app the previous release returned. Each is that app behind the lock check R29 states, so it reads the lock when it starts up and before each request it begins to serve; it is not an instance of the previous release's app's class, and code that checks that class sees the change. While the lock is on, neither is handed out and the MCP server does not start (R29), which this requirement allows.
Source: D249 (revision 28: a review left pending by an earlier release resumes and is decided, finalised and recorded on the figures this release computes; where a figure a policy rule reads differs, each rule's outcome comes from the stored policy result when it settles it, and otherwise from the policy file the review was drafted with, known by the hash stored with the review; when neither settles it, cma resume refuses, naming the figure and saying to run cma run on the statement again, with the stored review untouched and nothing recorded); D258 (revision 29: cma processes that share a checkpoint file serialise starting a review, refreshing a resumed one and applying a decision with a lock file beside it; another program writing to the checkpoint file, removing or replacing the lock file, and a process stopped part-way through a write are outside the single-decision guarantee; the memo and its evidence are made in full before either is written and are moved into place, evidence first; a disk failure, a full disk or an interruption after a decision is recorded and before both files are in place is outside the guarantee, the decision stays recorded in the checkpoint, and a second export of a decided review is outside this release); D289 (revision 30: with the offline lock off, the MCP server hands out its ASGI apps, sse_app and streamable_http_app, as the previous release did, each reading the lock at start-up and before every request; under the lock no app is handed out (R29)).

## R54 · conditional · risk: data
IF CMA_RULESET is set to v1, THEN THE SYSTEM SHALL categorise with the built-in rule table as it was before this change, SHALL give M1 to M16 the evidence the previous release gave them for every statement both accept that it categorises on its own with no case profile, as the previous release ran it, wherever the previous release's arithmetic was exact (R10, P2), and SHALL record v1 as the ruleset in every evidence record.
The previous release added amounts as binary floating-point numbers and rounded each result to the penny. Its arithmetic was exact on a statement when each money figure among M1 to M16 that it computed by adding amounts, total revenue (M2), debt service (M8), net operating cash flow (M9), owner drawings (M11), VAT paid (M12) and PAYE paid (M14), came, as it rounded it, to the exact pence R2 counts. Where one did not, R2's exact pence prevail: THE SYSTEM SHALL compute M1 to M16 from the exact pence, as R2 says, and SHALL cite each figure's evidence by the rule the previous release cited it by (R10), applied to the exact pence, so its lines can differ from the previous release's (with every loan repayment returned unpaid, debt service (M8) is £0.00 in exact pence, so debt service cover (M10) is n/a and cites no lines, though a previous release whose float sum left a fraction of a penny that it rounded to £0.01 of debt service cited every operating, drawings and loan line for M10). D234 places such statements at about a trillion pounds or more, and only on some Python versions; this requirement knows them by the outcome, not by a size. The dual run compares M1 to M16 with the previous release's on the statements on which the two agree.
A case profile, naming directors and shareholders (R5) or the applicant's other accounts (R6), is new behaviour under any ruleset, v1 included, and so is an own-account pairing between a case's accounts (R8). With either, a line a profile name or a pairing decides takes the category R5, R6 or R8 gives it, or is ambiguous where R5 and R6 say so, and M16 and M26 leave own-account transfers out of the money in they are a share of: a credit a profile name or a pairing makes an own-account transfer leaves the denominator and the evidence of both. M1 to M16 then cite their evidence by the rule the previous release cited it by (R10), and their lines can differ from the previous release's.
IF the table the system loads as v1 has bytes other than those whose hash the system pins in its own code, THEN THE SYSTEM SHALL refuse it as a changed frozen table, naming the table, before it checks anything else in the table, SHALL name after that what else keeps it from loading as R4 says, which for a table that is not well formed is the faults its loader finds, at least one, naming every fault being outside R4 and this requirement, and SHALL categorise nothing with it (R4).
Source: D153 (revision 18: v1 is known by the hash the system pins); D158 (revision 19: a changed v1 is refused as a changed frozen table before anything else is checked, and what else it breaks is named after that); D210 (revision 23: for a changed v1 that is not well formed, the faults its loader finds, at least one, are named after the change); D223 (revision 25: M1 to M16 keep the evidence the previous release gave them, which the dual run compares); D229 (revision 26: M1 to M16 keep the previous release's evidence for a statement categorised by v1 with no case profile, as the previous release ran; a case profile is new behaviour under any ruleset, and with one M16 and M26 exclude own-account transfers from the share they are a share of); D234 (revision 27: M1 to M16 keep the previous release's evidence wherever its arithmetic was exact; where its binary-float sums came out a penny away from the exact pence, R2's exact pence prevail and the figure and its evidence follow them, the dual run comparing the statements where both agree).

## P2 · property
For any sum figure, the signed amounts of its evidence lines SHALL total the figure to the penny; every money-in bucket, total money in (M17), cash deposits within trading receipts (M18), and each monthly money-in and trading-receipts cell is a sum figure. For any outflow figure, which is reported as a positive amount, the signed amounts of its evidence lines SHALL total the figure negated, to the penny; debt service (M8), owner drawings (M11), VAT paid (M12), PAYE paid (M14), each monthly money-out cell and every other figure the memo shows as money paid out is an outflow figure. A derived figure, such as net operating cash flow (M9), an average, share, ratio, count or lowest balance, or a monthly balance or overdrawn-day cell, is not a sum figure, and this property does not ask its evidence lines' amounts to total it. Each derived figure among M1 to M16 SHALL cite its evidence by the rule the previous release, the package before this change, cited it by (R10), except that M16, like M26, leaves own-account transfers out of the money in it is a share of (R54); for a statement categorised by v1 on its own with no case profile, where no line is an own-account transfer, each SHALL cite the lines the previous release cited wherever the previous release's arithmetic was exact, which the dual run compares (R54); where it was not, each SHALL cite its lines by that rule applied to R2's exact pence, so debt service cover (M10) cites no lines when the exact debt service (M8) is £0.00, whatever the previous release's float sum gave (R2, R54). By that rule, lowest balance (M5) SHALL cite the line that set it, and days overdrawn (M6) the line that closed each overdrawn day, a day with no lines taking the line that set the balance carried into it, and not the lines that closed the days it read and found in credit. Every other derived figure, among them the unexplained share (M26), average end-of-day balance (M27), debt service cover including the proposed facility (M28) and each monthly balance and overdrawn-day cell, SHALL cite every line its calculation reads: for a figure read from end-of-day balances, the line that set the close of every day it reads, in the period or the month, whether or not that day decides the figure, a day with no lines taking the line that set the balance carried into it; and for a share or ratio, every line counted in its numerator or denominator. Net operating cash flow (M9) SHALL name, in a note carried with its evidence, each of its evidence lines that counts only in part: a credit from someone the business paid by transfer, which offsets no more than was sent to them.
Validates: R1, R2, R10, R11, R16, R54, R55

## P15 · property
For any case log, read in any window, command or thread and through any CaseLog object or path to the case folder: the holder of the case, sign-off included, and any reader that finds the case free SHALL report any changed, removed or reordered entry as broken and an incomplete last entry as incomplete, naming the entry; while another writer holds the case, a reader SHALL report a change, removal or reordering at or before the recorded entries as broken, naming the entry, judged from a steady read, SHALL report what follows them as being saved, never as intact and never as broken, and SHALL give the recorded entries, and the rest as being saved, only from a steady read through which one writer held the case, which it knows when it held the gate through the read; with the gate busy and the case lock held, it SHALL name only a change, removal or reordering at or before the recorded entries that a steady read shows, and otherwise claim nothing; and with no steady read within its retries, or with every attempt ending in that corner with no such fault, it SHALL claim nothing about any entry. A lock the reader's own thread holds through another CaseLog object or path, and a lock that cannot be taken or tested, are not another holder. A reader's read SHALL count only while the lock files it counts on are still the case's lock files: the case lock it holds and the gate it passed, for the holder of the case, sign-off included; the case lock it holds shared, for a reader that finds the gate busy; and the gate it holds through the read and the case lock behind it, for any other reader. A read across one that was removed or replaced SHALL be taken again, by the holder as a reader that does not hold the case, and nothing SHALL be claimed from it, and with every attempt within the retries read so, nothing SHALL be claimed about any entry. A reader SHALL learn whether a lock file it counts on was removed or replaced during its read from a check after the read that looks at each lock file it counts on and then, where it counts on two, at the first again; the holder of the case, sign-off included, SHALL also check that each lock file it holds still has a name. Lock-file interference beyond a read across a lock file the reader counts on is outside what a reader can know. So, since a lock-file check is a sequence of looks at the file system, is a lock file removed or replaced, renamed aside and replaced included, after the check's last look at that file, within the check's own last look. A check made after the interference stops gives the true answer. For any two case-log writers on one case, whether two processes, two threads sharing one CaseLog object or two CaseLog objects in one process, at most one SHALL hold the case at any moment, and the holder SHALL run one save at a time: a save begun inside another on the same thread SHALL be refused before it writes anything, and a save SHALL be refused when the log grew since its check. Scope: case folders on a local disk; no fork while the case-log lock is held, and a forked child's writes through an inherited CaseLog are refused. Scope (revision 10; the steps named in revision 12; the repair boundary in revision 13; the take-back and its third answer in revision 17; a refusal partway through the line in revision 19; no report of nothing written once the line is in the log in revision 20): the case lock files are the tool's own; removing, renaming or replacing either while the case is in use is outside the at-most-one-holder guarantee, as hand-editing the log is. A writer SHALL re-check its lock before each step that changes a file a reader reads (the empty head record written before the first entry's line, each entry's line, and each rename that publishes a head record or a repaired log, a take-back's included; writing a temporary file that nobody reads is not such a step) and SHALL refuse the step if the case lock file was removed or replaced. A writer refused once any part of its line is written, and before its head record is published, SHALL report its save as not recorded, never as nothing written, and the part written, the whole line or less, stays past the recorded entries as a torn entry, as a crash leaves it. Once any part of a save's line is in the log, which for a repair is once its repaired log has been renamed over the log, no part of what the save's writer is told, the words of any refusal or error it carries included, SHALL say that nothing was written, whatever its answer. A save whose read-back fails SHALL take back the head record it published while that head record is still its own, so that its entry stays past the recorded entries and the save is reported as not recorded; a save whose take-back is not made SHALL be reported as not confirmed, neither recorded nor not recorded, and SHALL ask for the case log to be checked before anything else is saved. Under such interference the tool tries to leave a log reported broken as R43 says, never intact, when two writers were each told that a save at the same log position was recorded, and a log that reads intact only when it holds the entry of every save whose writer was told it was recorded; since revision 23 these are what it tries, not guarantees (below). A log that lacks only the entry of a save reported as not recorded or not confirmed, which the next holder's repair removed once a person confirmed it, lost no recorded save. Scope (revision 23): removing, renaming or replacing a case lock file while the case is in use, and interrupting a save at an arbitrary instant (a signal, Ctrl-C, or a handler, callback or finaliser that runs inside the save, a nested save from one included), are outside every guarantee of this property, as hand-editing the log is: under them THE SYSTEM keeps the clauses above as far as it can, reporting broken, being saved, not recorded or not confirmed as it can, and a check made after the interference stops gives the true answer. A save begun inside another on the same thread is still refused before it writes anything. With no such interference, every clause of this property holds as written.
Validates: R42, R43
