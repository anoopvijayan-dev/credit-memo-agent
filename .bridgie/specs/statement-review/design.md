# Design — Statement review: every figure traced, the unclear lines first

Revision 5, 2026-11-19, after D65 (changes requested; the owner raised it as D63 after the fifth T12 review). What changed from revision 4: the case log's concurrency scope is stated where R42, R43 and P15 sit, and their ids do not change. One writer at a time holds a case, across windows, commands, threads and separate CaseLog objects in one process. A reader never excuses a torn or tampered log as another writer's append; the design reads that as excusing nothing except an append in progress while another writer holds the case, and puts that reading to the owner. Forking a process while it holds the case-log lock is not supported (the tool never forks), and a forked child's writes through an inherited CaseLog are refused. Case folders must be on a local disk. The design also takes in D64, the owner's guidance after T03's sixth review: a listed phrase is found wherever it starts, even inside another match of the same rule, and a combining mark joins the word it follows. The changes are in Context, two new sub-decisions, C1, C3, C5, C6, the traceability table, Out of scope, Consequences, the perspective check, the case root layout, the CLI and workspace API, Failure modes, Migration, Rollout, Observability, Security notes and Consumer trace item 20. Every other requirement, property and id is unchanged. The owner answered revision 4's question with the design default (D61) and approved it (D62).

Earlier revisions. Revision 4 (2026-11-18, after D60) moved R4's queue clause to R15, so that T03, which builds the categoriser, can complete R4; it also put the rail-plus-cash check ahead of the cues, made words whole in any script, and declared the category order inside a bucket. Revision 3 (after D52) gave R4 its ambiguity policy; the owner kept its defaults (D53, D54) and approved it (D55). Revision 2 (2026-11-16, after D34) counted cash deposits as trading receipts and flagged the large ones (D31), showed pass/refer/fail with an illustrative label (D28), kept timing out of the tool (D32) and wrote the pack once, at sign-off. The owner confirmed local-first (D29), OBIE v3.1 and TrueLayer first (D30) and Tier 1 only by 2026-11-29 (D33).

## Context

**The bet as signed.** One underwriter at a UK lender (D1) reads months of business bank statements by hand before writing each credit memo. The firm bans generic AI, and the friend's fear is a made-up number on a file they sign. The owner chose to treat this as a showcase for one firm (D15, D24): build a working model, show it, then ask the firm's approver for a pilot (D14, D25). The demo runs on the owner's own statements, as a demonstration of the mechanism rather than of the time saving (D23).

**What the evidence changed.** The underwriter's written reply of 2026-11-16 is one anecdotal source and possibly templated (evidence-synthesis.md). It moves the value from adding up to judging:
- 25-45 of a 45-60 minute review goes on 'unusual transactions, internal transfers, director loans, cash deposits'. Adding up takes 10-20.
- They want to click any number and see its transactions, correct categories themselves, get a summary they can paste into their memo, and keep the lending decision.
- The pilot blockers they expect are data protection and accuracy. A tool that runs on the firm's own machine 'could be attractive', and IT/compliance must approve it.
- Formats: an estimated 9 of 15 files are PDF, 4 Open Banking and 2 mixed. This is an estimate, not a count.

**What the owner decided on revision 1** (D28-D35, 2026-11-16)
- D31: cash deposits count as trading receipts, and deposits above a threshold are flagged. Revision 1 kept every cash deposit out of trading receipts until a person moved it.
- D28: pass/refer/fail is shown, labelled as illustrative thresholds, not the firm's policy. Revision 1 hid it unless the firm loaded its own policy.
- D32: no timing in the tool; files are timed with a stopwatch. R49 is withdrawn and R50 reports no durations.
- D29, D30, D33: the design defaults stand: local-first, OBIE v3.1 and TrueLayer before Plaid or Yapily, Tier 1 only by 2026-11-29.
- D35: the shipped cash limits are £2,500 for a single deposit and 50% of a month's trading receipts.

**What T03's first four reviews showed, and what the owner decided** (D46-D52, 2026-11-18)
- T03 builds the v2 tables and the categoriser for R4. It failed review four times. Each round fixed the cases found, and the next round found a new pair of lists where rule order, or one rule's exception, picked a bucket without telling anyone. First, CAPITAL in a savings account's name or a merchant's name made money director funding (D46). Next came a counter deposit at a branch with CAPITAL in its name, a bare TAX POT label in a refund, and SAVINGS in a compensation payer's name (D47). Last came HMRC credits with savings wording, cash at a branch whose name holds a generic explained word, a bare pot label in an external payment, and a refund from a merchant named with savings wording (D49).
- D46: classify in two stages, explicit line-type wording before the name lists, and the line-type cue wins.
- D47: a line whose matching rules span two or more buckets goes to the judgement queue with every candidate recorded.
- D48: bare words are not evidence. CAPITAL counts only in phrases (capital introduced, injected or contributed). A move between own accounts needs move wording or an account named in the profile, so bare SAVINGS stays unexplained. Generic explained words are a name list below explicit cues.
- D52 puts the policy into R4: a credit whose wording is ambiguous or conflicting goes to the judgement queue and is never counted as trading receipts.
- The lesson: the defect was never one missing word. The categoriser settled disagreement between lists by order, and each fix steered one more pair. Revision 3 makes disagreement a verdict of its own, which the underwriter sees.

**What T03's fifth review showed, and what the owner decided** (D53-D60, 2026-11-18)
- D53 and D54 kept revision 3's defaults: cues that agree outrank names, and only an override, a profile name or a pairing settles an ambiguous credit, never a firm rule. D55 approved revision 3, and D49 sent T03 back to rebuild the verdict as C1 said.
- The fifth attempt (verify-32) passed 644 tests and failed R4 on four major findings. A cue with a bucket returned before the rail-plus-cash check, so BACS CREDIT HMRC CASH PAID IN became an HMRC repayment. Candidates came only from the stage that decided, so FPS CREDIT HMRC TO SAVINGS STRIPE recorded the savings and HMRC rules but not the rail or the gateway. `cma run` showed no judgement queue. Word boundaries counted only ASCII letters, so STRIPE inside CAFÉSTRIPE LTD counted as trading receipts. A minor finding added that the category inside the trading bucket followed rule order, and the round-trip check reads the category, so reordering rules could move a credit out of M2.
- D58 and D60: R4 asks only for what the categoriser does: mark the credit, place it in unexplained money in, and record every rule it matched in either stage. Listing it in the judgement queue moves to R15, whose task builds the queue, and R15 now also shows each candidate's bucket.
- Revision 4 settles the other findings in C1: the rail-plus-cash check runs before the cues decide, words are whole in any script, and a declared category order picks the category inside a bucket. A queue in `cma run`'s terminal review is out of scope.

**What T03's sixth review showed, and what the owner decided** (D64, 2026-11-19)
- The sixth attempt (verify-35) passed 688 tests, with the rail-plus-cash check, stage precedence and both-stage candidates in place, and failed R4 on two major findings. A rule's phrases were searched one after another without overlap, so a longer phrase that starts inside a shorter match of the same rule was missed: in FROM POT TRANSFER CASH DEPOSIT, FROM POT hid POT TRANSFER, and the leftover TRANSFER counted as a payment rail and made the credit ambiguous. And word boundaries treated combining marks outside a few hard-coded ranges as separators, so STRIPE after a Devanagari vowel sign counted as a gateway and as trading receipts. Revision 4's own wording (a boundary is anything that is not a letter or a digit) allowed the second. A minor finding added that debit categories still follow rule order, which the design intends.
- D64: the owner took T03 over: find every place a listed phrase starts, overlaps included, and decide word boundaries with Unicode's own character categories, a combining mark joining the word. C1 now says both, and says that the rule-order shuffle covers credits only.

**What T12's reviews showed, and what the owner decided** (D50-D65, 2026-11-18 to 2026-11-19)
- T12 builds the hash-chained case log and its single-writer lock for P15; R42 and R43 complete later, when sign-off (T27) and the other writers record into it. Detection passed from the start. Every failed round was about concurrency, and each found a new corner: a lock error read as another writer, which excused a torn entry (D50); a failed unlock left the lock held (D51); a forked child entered, or released, its parent's lock (D56, D57, D59); then, in the fifth review (verify-34), concurrent forks raced inside the fork hooks themselves, and a second handle opened through a symlinked folder took its own thread's lock for another writer's and reported a torn log as intact.
- D63: scope it, simplify, take over. Forking a process while it holds the case-log lock is not supported, because the tool never forks, and the fork machinery is removed. A child writing through an inherited CaseLog is refused, and leaving a writer block it inherited releases nothing. Locks are recognised by the lock file itself (its device and inode), not by the path's spelling, so a symlinked path is the same case.
- D65: state that scope where R42, R43 and P15 sit, without changing their ids: one writer at a time per case across windows, commands, threads and separate CaseLog objects in one process; a reader never excuses a torn or tampered log as another writer's append; no fork while the lock is held; case folders on a local disk.
- The lesson is T03's again: when a requirement leaves its scope open, each review explores the next corner of it. Writing down what is supported, and what is not, ends the search, and the corners outside it become stated limits.

## Options considered

**Option A: local review workspace (chosen).** The cma package serves a web page on the underwriter's machine, reachable only from that machine (loopback). Every figure, bucket and monthly cell opens its lines, and the lines needing judgement come first. The underwriter re-categorises lines with a reason and the figures layer recomputes, along with the indicators and the illustrative policy check. A named sign-off writes a memo-ready pack and a self-contained HTML record. The model is off by default.

**Option B: static HTML report plus a CLI overrides file.** Each run writes one self-contained HTML file with click-through. Corrections are typed into an overrides file and the run repeated; sign-off stays at the terminal. This is the cheapest option and needs no server. *It lost because* the two things the underwriter asked for beyond click-through, correcting categories and a usable summary, would happen in a file-editing loop outside the page, and the underwriter would see the effect of a correction on the figures and the policy check only after a re-run. It is kept as the format of the signed record (the pack's HTML).

**Option C: spreadsheet as the interface.** Export lines and figures to a workbook, let the underwriter edit a category column, and re-import. It is familiar: they add things up in a spreadsheet today. *It lost because* a figure loses its link to its evidence as soon as a cell is edited or copied. The tool also cannot tell a category correction from an edited amount, and re-importing a hand-edited workbook is the error-prone step the bet is meant to remove. A monthly-summary CSV stays as an export.

**Option D: hosted service (SaaS) now.** One hosted web app per firm; D14 frames the business as SaaS. *It lost for this bet because* applicant data would leave the firm, which is the blocker the reply names. A hosted pilot also needs sign-in, tenancy and a data processing agreement before the approver will look at it. Option A's workspace is the same web page, so hosting it later is a change of deployment, not a rewrite. The owner confirmed local-first (D29).

**Option E: let a model categorise the unclear lines.** Send unexplained lines to a model and accept the buckets it suggests. *It lost because* it puts payer-written text and names in a prompt, which raises GDPR and prompt-injection risk from whoever wrote the payment reference. It also breaks ADR 001 ('the model receives a table, not a statement'), and a category chosen by a model is exactly the kind of invented judgement the underwriter fears. Deterministic rules, rules the firm learns and per-line overrides do this job instead.

**Sub-decision: how to show what is unexplained.** (i) Redefine M16 to mean unexplained only. (ii) Keep M16's value (money in that is not trading receipts), rename it to say so, and add a figure for each bucket plus the unexplained share (M26). *Chose (ii)*: a memo already signed that cites M16 must keep its meaning, and ids are stable.

**Sub-decision: how cash deposits count (D31).** (i) Out of trading receipts until the underwriter moves them (revision 1). (ii) In trading receipts, with the cash inside them reported as its own figure (M18) and indicators on large deposits, cash-heavy months and cash withdrawn then paid back in. (iii) A firm setting that chooses (i) or (ii). *Chose (ii)*, the owner's answer. (i) lost because it understates a cash business such as a café and makes the underwriter add back every ordinary deposit by hand. (iii) lost because M2 would then mean different things in different packs, so a signed memo citing M2 could not be read without knowing the setting; a firm that wants a deposit out moves it with an override and a reason. What (ii) gives up: personal cash paid in by an owner lifts M2, M9 and M10 unless an indicator catches it or a person moves it (see Consequences).

**Sub-decision: how the policy outcome is shown (D28).** (i) Hidden unless the firm loads its own policy (revision 1). (ii) Shown from the packaged policy in the workspace and the pack, with the illustrative label inside the same panel and the same pack section, behind a firm setting that is on by default. (iii) Removed from the review flow; `cma run` keeps it. *Chose (ii)*, the owner's answer. Putting the label in the same section, not in a footnote or only in the HTML record, means copying the section copies the label. (i) lost because the firm has no policy file to load (E8 is open), so the outcome would stay hidden for the whole showcase. (iii) lost because the showcase would no longer show the whole chain, from a traced figure to a rule result that a person must act on.

**Sub-decision: how sign-off works.** (i) Drive the existing LangGraph review interrupt from the workspace. (ii) Make the case folder the source of truth, and make sign-off a case-log event bound to a snapshot hash. *Chose (ii)*. The figures are a pure function of the case's inputs, so a checkpoint would hold nothing extra, and re-running the graph after every override would also re-run the draft node. ReviewDecision's name and notes validation is reused, not copied. `cma run` keeps its interrupt unchanged. Revision 2 settled the order: the underwriter signs a preview, and the pack is written once, at sign-off, from that preview.

**Sub-decision: where the categorisation rules live.** (i) Keep them as code and add more. (ii) Move the rule tables into packaged, validated data files like the policy YAML; the matching passes (reversals, round trips, pairing, profile names) stay in code. *Chose (ii)*: the lists of acquirers, lenders, cash wording and gambling operators are what changes most often, the v1 table can be kept as data for rollback, and a firm's own rules use the same format.

**Sub-decision: how the categoriser settles wording that points two ways (D52, revision 3).** (i) First match in table order, with exceptions inside rules to steer known collisions: v1, and T03's attempts before revision 3. (ii) Two stages, with disagreement as a verdict of its own: line-type cues decide when any match; names decide only when no cue matches; within the deciding stage, matches in two or more buckets make the credit ambiguous. A match inside a longer listed phrase counts only as the longer one, and the bank's own returned-payment code settles the line type alone. (iii) Any two buckets anywhere, cue or name, make the credit ambiguous. *Chose (ii)*. (i) lost because order is a silent choice. Each review round found a new pair of lists it got wrong, and an exception that hides one list's cue when another's is present (HMRC hidden by savings wording) turns a disagreement into a guess nobody sees. (iii) lost because a cue says what the line is and a name says who sent it. Every refund from PayPal, Amazon or an acquirer, and every bounced direct debit to HMRC, would go to the queue though its wording is not in doubt, and it would undo the owner's D46 rule that the cue wins. What (ii) gives up: a cue in payer-written text outranks names, so a payer who writes REFUND moves their credit to refunds. Every cue is non-trading, so this can only understate trading receipts, never invent them. The owner confirmed this reading (D53).

**Sub-decision: what settles an ambiguous credit (revision 3).** (i) Only a per-line override. (ii) A per-line override, a case-profile name or an own-account pairing; never a firm rule. (iii) Anything that ranks above the built-in table's unprotected rules, firm rules included. *Chose (ii)*, which the owner confirmed (D54). (i) contradicts R5, R6 and R8, which say a credit from a director or own account named in the profile, or a paired transfer, SHALL take that category; the owner asked to keep them. Profile names and pairing only ever give non-trading buckets, so R4's rule that an ambiguous credit is never trading receipts holds. (iii) lost because a firm rule can name trading receipts, and it was previewed on another case's lines, not on this one. It would count ambiguous wording as trading without a person looking at the line.

**Sub-decision: what an ambiguous credit records as its candidates (D60, revision 4).** (i) The matches of the stage that decided (revision 3's reading, and T03's fifth attempt). (ii) Every rule the credit matched in either stage, after a match inside a longer phrase counts as the longer one, the payment rail included. *Chose (ii)*, the owner's answer. (i) lost because the underwriter would not see a name that the cues outranked: FPS CREDIT HMRC TO SAVINGS STRIPE would show HMRC and savings and hide the gateway, which is the reason a person might call it trading. What (ii) costs: longer lists, and a rail candidate that gives no bucket, which the queue shows as such.

**Sub-decision: where the judgement queue is required (D60, revision 4).** (i) In R4, so the categoriser's task also had to show the queue (revision 3). (ii) In R15, the case view, whose task builds the queue; R4 requires only what the categoriser produces. (iii) In both R4 and R15. *Chose (ii)*, the owner's answer. (i) lost because T03 could not complete R4 without the workspace, which depends on T03 (the fifth review failed it for that). (iii) lost for the same reason. What (ii) leaves out: `cma run` is not a case and shows no queue; its evidence lines carry the conflict and candidates (Out of scope).

**Sub-decision: which category a credit gets inside one bucket (revision 4).** Two rules can agree on the bucket but carry different categories: a gateway (card settlement) and an invoice reference (customer transfer) are both trading receipts; a returned-payment cue and a refund cue are both refunds and returned payments. The category matters downstream: the round-trip check undoes only customer transfers, M15 reads customer transfers, M7, reversal linking and the returned-DD indicator read returned payments, the M16 numerator reads refunds, and M18 and the cash indicators read cash deposits. (i) The first rule in table order (revision 3): reordering rules then changes M2, M7 and M15, which the fifth review showed. (ii) Make it ambiguous: R4 defines ambiguity by bucket, so this would amend R4 and fill the queue with credits whose bucket is not in doubt. (iii) A category order declared with the table and applied once the bucket is known, putting first the category that brings more checks. *Chose (iii)*, which the owner confirmed (D61): in trading receipts, cash deposit, then customer transfer, then card settlement; in refunds and returned payments, returned payment, then refund. A payer cannot avoid the round-trip check by adding a gateway's name, or the cash flags by adding an invoice reference. What (iii) costs: a gateway payout that carries an invoice reference counts as a named customer in M15. The order is data in the table and is hashed with it.

**Sub-decision: how far the case log's concurrency goes (D63, D65, revision 5).** (i) Support every way a process could share a case: forked children, network shares, any mix of handles (T12's attempts before D63, with fork hooks and per-acquisition tokens). (ii) State a scope and refuse what falls outside it: one writer at a time per case across windows, commands, threads and separate CaseLog objects in one process, on a local disk, with no fork while the lock is held. (iii) Hand out one shared CaseLog per case in each process, so separate objects never meet. *Chose (ii)*, the owner's answer. (i) lost because the tool never forks, yet the fork machinery cost three review rounds, and the fifth review's first finding was a race inside the fork hooks themselves. (iii) lost because it holds only if every caller goes through the shared handle: a test or a later command that builds its own CaseLog would bypass it, while the operating-system lock separates handles whichever code built them, on a local disk. What (ii) gives up: on a network share the operating system may emulate the lock per process, so two CaseLog objects in one process might both write, and nothing detects a share (owner question). A workspace that forked worker processes would also fall outside the scope, so C5 serves from one process.

**Sub-decision: what a reader may report while another writer holds the case (D65, revision 5).** A writer adds its entry and then records it as complete; a reader that looks in between sees an incomplete last entry. (i) Report what it reads: every reader calls that entry incomplete, even while another window's append is landing, so a second window shows a broken-log warning whenever the first one saves, and asks the underwriter to drop a live append. (ii) Excuse nothing except while another writer holds the case, and then only what follows the last entry recorded as complete: such a reader reports the recorded entries and an append in progress; anything wrong at or before them is reported whoever holds the case; a reader that holds the case, or finds it free, reports an incomplete entry as incomplete; a lock the reader's own thread holds through another handle or path, and a lock that cannot be taken or tested, are not another writer. (iii) Wait for the lock before reporting: never a mistaken reading, but every window stalls behind a writer, and a stuck writer stalls them all. *Chose (ii)*, what T12 builds, as the design's reading of D65, put to the owner. What (ii) gives up: while another writer holds the case, a reader cannot tell a torn entry left by a crash from a live append, so for that moment it shows an append in progress. It never shows the entry as complete, the next look after the writer releases reports it incomplete, and sign-off and every append decide as writers holding the case, so neither can pass it.

## Decision

Build Option A as components inside the existing package. ADR 001 still holds: money is added up only in the figures layer, and the model never sees a statement.

          |
          v
    loader and validation -> categoriser (rule tables, verdict, profile, firm rules, overrides)

**Components**
- **C1 Rule tables and categoriser** (src/cma/categorise.py; rule tables as data under src/cma/rules/). Built-in tables v1 (today's, frozen, first match wins) and v2 (the new lists, in two stages). v2 names cash paid in (counter, deposit machine, Post Office and PayPoint wording) as cash_deposit, and names cash-withdrawal wording that only the cash round-trip indicator reads. The ambiguity policy (R4) applies to credits under v2. Debits keep first-match order through the same stages: a debit that two rules match takes the first in table order, so the rule-order shuffle (Rollout) compares credits only. v1 is untouched (R54). Each line records which rule set its category.
  - *Stage 1, line-type cues*: explicit wording of what the line is. A returned payment, a move to or from the applicant's own pot, space or savings, HMRC, a refund or reversal, and the payment rail (Faster Payment, BACS, CHAPS, a bank giro credit, a transfer, a standing order). The rail has no bucket of its own; it says the money did not arrive as cash.
  - *Stage 2, name lists*: who sent the money or what it is for. Cash paid-in wording; lenders and merchant cash advance providers; director, capital-introduced and shareholder wording; generic loan wording; acquirers, gateways, marketplaces and delivery platforms; invoice references; explained non-trading words (interest, grant, cashback, compensation, insurance claim).
  - *Whole words* (revision 4, sharpened in revision 5 by D64): a listed word or phrase, and a name from the case profile, matches only where the character on each side is not a letter, a digit or a combining mark in any script; a combining mark belongs to the word it follows. CAFÉSTRIPE LTD does not contain STRIPE, whether the É is one character or an E followed by a combining accent, and neither does किSTRIPE, whose vowel sign joins the word. The words of a phrase may be separated by any run of spaces. Rules and profile names share this one definition.
  - *Every occurrence* (revision 5, D64): a listed phrase is found wherever it starts, including inside another match of the same rule. In FROM POT TRANSFER CASH DEPOSIT the own-pot rule matches both FROM POT and POT TRANSFER, and the longer POT TRANSFER takes in TRANSFER, so no payment rail is left over and the own-account cue decides.
  - *The verdict on a credit*, in this order:
    1. Collect every credit rule that matches, from both stages, at every position where its wording occurs, overlapping occurrences included. A match inside a longer match from another rule counts only as the longer one (STRIPE inside STRIPE CAPITAL, LOAN inside DIRECTOR LOAN, REFUND inside HMRC REFUND, TRANSFER inside POT TRANSFER). What remains are the credit's matches.
    2. A returned-payment code the bank prints at the start of the line settles the line type on its own, because it is the bank's code, not payer wording.
    3. A payment rail together with cash paid-in wording makes the credit ambiguous, whatever other cue matches (BACS CREDIT HMRC CASH PAID IN): the line says both that the money came by transfer and that it came as cash.
    4. Otherwise, if any cue with a bucket matches, the cues decide: cues in one bucket give that bucket, and cues in two or more buckets make the credit ambiguous.
    5. With no such cue, the names decide in the same way. The rail alone decides nothing. If no name matches either, nothing is decided, and the line falls through the precedence below to firm rules and the default.
    6. Inside the bucket decided, the category is the first in the table's declared category order that a deciding match carries: in trading receipts, cash deposit, then customer transfer, then card settlement; in refunds and returned payments, returned payment, then refund. Every other bucket has one category. The rule recorded is the first deciding match, in table order, that carries that category.
    7. An ambiguous credit's candidates are all of its matches from step 1, from both stages and the rail included, written in table order. Its category is other (unexplained).
  - *Bare words are not evidence* (D48): SAVINGS, SAVER, POT, SPACE or a pot label such as TAX POT without move wording, and CAPITAL outside capital introduced, injected or contributed, match no list.
  - *No hidden tie-breaks*: a rule's own exceptions may exclude wording that is on no list (a cheque is not cash), but never another list's cue or name. Where two lists' wording meets, either a longer phrase goes on one list, or the credit is ambiguous.
  - *Precedence*, highest first: (1) a per-line override; (2) a protected verdict, meaning the line-type cues plus lender, director, capital-introduced, shareholder and generic loan wording (R21's patterns and the cues); (3) a case-profile name (R5, R6) or an own-account pairing (R8); (4) an ambiguous verdict, which puts the credit in unexplained money in with every candidate recorded; (5) firm rules, which therefore never reach a line settled at (2) or (4) (R21, R4); (6) an unprotected verdict: the trading lists, invoice references, cash paid in and explained non-trading words; (7) the default, where a debit is supplier and a credit is unexplained.
  - Cash paid in is not protected (revision 2 never listed it), so a director named in the profile outranks cash wording, and a director's cash is director funding (R5). Every protected verdict, and every category a profile name or a pairing gives, is non-trading. So the only routes into trading receipts are an unambiguous trading verdict, a firm rule on a line that steps (2) to (4) did not settle, and a person's override.
  - *What C1 hands on* (revision 4): each line's category and rule, an ambiguous credit's conflict form and candidates included, and, for any rule id of a table, the category that rule sets (none for the rail). C1 does not build the judgement queue; C2 assembles it and C5 shows it (R15).
- **C2 Figures layer** (src/cma/metrics.py, new src/cma/indicators.py). Adds M17-M28, the monthly summary and the indicators, including the three cash indicators. cash_deposit is a trading-receipts category, so M2 and every figure built on it include cash, and M18 reports the cash inside M2. All sums and threshold comparisons are in integer pence. Each figure comes with its evidence and, for sums, the evidence total. A case whose buckets do not reconcile is refused. C2 also assembles the judgement queue as a pure function of the categorised lines, the pairing candidates and the indicators (R15): unexplained lines, ambiguous credits among them, possible own-account transfers and every flagged line, largest amount first. An ambiguous credit's entry carries its candidates, each with the category C1 says its rule sets and the bucket that category maps to.
- **C3 Case store** (new src/cma/case.py). Case root, case folders, profile, override log, the hash-chained case log with its head record and single-writer lock, snapshot hash, purge and purge ledger, and the git-ignore and sync-folder checks.
  - *Concurrency scope* (revision 5; R42, R43, P15): one writer at a time holds a case, whether the writers are windows, commands, threads or separate CaseLog objects in one process. A writer that finds the case held is refused at once, never queued, and one whose expected log position is stale gets a conflict and reloads (R17). The lock belongs to a thread of a process, not to an object, and is recognised by the lock file itself, so two paths to one case folder, a symlink among them, are one case. The operating system drops the lock when its holder dies, so a crash never leaves a case locked.
  - *What a reader reports*: anything wrong at or before the last entry recorded as complete, whoever holds the case. An incomplete last entry is reported as incomplete by a reader that holds the case or finds it free; a reader that finds another writer holding it reports the recorded entries and an append in progress. A lock the reader's own thread holds through another handle or path, and a lock that cannot be taken or tested, are not another writer (D50). Sign-off and every append check the log as writers holding the case.
  - *Not supported, stated rather than detected*: forking a process while it holds a case's lock (the tool never forks; a forked child's writes through the inherited CaseLog are refused, and the child leaving a writer block releases nothing of its parent's); and case folders on a network share, where the operating system may emulate the lock per process and so not separate two CaseLog objects in one process.
- **C4 Importers** (src/cma/importers/). A shared step that walks and verifies the balance, extracted from monzo.py; new OBIE v3.1, TrueLayer and bank-CSV importers. Every importer writes the five-column file that ingest.load_csv validates.
- **C5 Review workspace** (new src/cma/review/). A loopback server and one bundled page with no build step. The server is one process that serves requests on threads and never forks workers, so every window's writes stay inside the case log's scope (R42). It renders what C2 and policy.evaluate computed and never computes money. The override form offers only categories valid for the line's direction. While show_policy_verdict is on, a policy-check panel shows the outcome and each rule's result on the current figures, with the illustrative label in the panel. The judgement queue comes first (R15). For an ambiguous credit it shows each candidate rule and the bucket it would give, and a rail candidate as giving no bucket, so the underwriter's override starts from the candidates. The drill-down shows each line's rule as recorded, which for an ambiguous credit is the conflict form (R16).
- **C6 Preview, pack and sign-off** (new src/cma/pack.py). Memo field map, deterministic section text, the labelled policy-check section, grounding guard, HTML record, evidence JSON v1, monthly CSV, sign-off binding to the preview's snapshot, and an atomic write of the pack once, at sign-off. Sign-off checks the case log and appends its entry inside one hold of the case, so a broken or incomplete log refuses it, however the log looked to a reader a moment before (R43).
- **C7 Firm configuration** (new src/cma/firm.py). offline_lock (default on), show_policy_verdict (default on, D28), and the paths to firm rules, thresholds and field map. There is no timing setting (D32).
- **C8 Verify** (new src/cma/verify.py). Recomputes an evidence record from its case and refuses records from another ruleset.
- **C9 Pseudonymiser** (new src/cma/pseudonymise.py).
- **C10 Synthetic data and evals** (src/cma/synth.py, evals/run_evals.py). New opt-in features, cash included, with truth for every bucket, line and indicator.
- **C11 Report** (CLI over C3). A local per-case summary with no personal data and no durations.
- **C12 Existing paths** (graph.py, cli.py, mcp_server.py, llm.py, policy.py). Behaviour unchanged; new fields are additive; the offline lock is checked. policy.evaluate and PolicyResult are reused as they are.

## Consequences

**Gains**
- The underwriter gets the view they asked for: £80k in, of which £55k is trading and the other £25k is explained by bucket. Every figure is clickable, and their own corrections are on record.
- A cash business is not understated: takings paid in as cash count as trading, the cash inside them is its own figure, and the deposits a person should look at come first (D31).
- No silent choice between lists: when the shipped lists disagree about a credit, the underwriter sees the line, every rule it matched in either stage and the bucket each would give, so a name the cues outranked is still in view (D60). Neither a credit's bucket nor its category depends on the order of rules within a stage, which a test checks by shuffling them and recategorising whole statements (D52, revision 4); a debit takes the first matching rule in table order, as it always has.
- Each task can now finish its own requirement: T03 delivers R4 through `cma run`'s evidence, and the queue's tasks deliver R15 (D58, D60).
- The case log has a stated boundary (D65): which writers it separates, what a reader may report while another writer holds the case, and what is not supported. Its review judges it against that boundary instead of searching for the next corner, and the fork machinery that cost three rounds is gone (D63).
- The showcase runs the whole chain, from traced figures to an illustrative pass, refer or fail that names the rules a person must look at, and the label says whose decision it is (D28).
- The approver's question, does data leave the firm, has an answer the code enforces: offline lock on, the MCP server refuses to start, and the evidence states offline mode.
- Firm rules build up in a file the firm owns. That is the only switching cost any board lens found.
- The bet's outcome is read by stopwatch on comparable files (D32). For each case, the report adds the size of the judgement queue, the indicator counts and the overrides by kind. Those explain the minutes without the tool measuring the person.

**Costs and risks**
- The scope is large for two weeks and $20, and Tier 1 alone is most of it. T03 has used its six attempts and is with the owner (D64); T12 has used its three and is with the owner (D63).
- A server, even one reachable only from the same machine, is new attack surface (see Security notes).
- Twelve more figures make the `cma run` prompt longer and the appendix wider.
- Cash paid in by the owner now counts as trading receipts. It lifts M2, M9 and M10, and can turn an illustrative fail into a pass (D31). The single-deposit, monthly-share and round-trip indicators catch the common patterns. They miss personal cash split into deposits below the limit, in months where cash stays under the share limit. The underwriter sees that only in M18 and the cash lines.
- More lines in the judgement queue (D52). A genuine cash deposit at a branch whose name holds a listed word, or a card settlement whose reference names a lender, sits in unexplained money in, outside M2, M9 and M18, until the underwriter moves it. The policy can understate trading receipts; it never overstates them.
- Longer candidate lists (D60). An ambiguous credit lists every rule it matched in either stage, the rail among them. A candidate's bucket can be one the deciding stage outranked, such as trading receipts for a gateway name beside two disagreeing cues; it is shown so the underwriter can choose it, never counted.
- The category order inside a bucket (revision 4) sends a gateway payout that carries an invoice reference through the round-trip check and counts it as a named customer in M15, and counts a line with both returned-payment and refund wording as a returned direct debit in M7. Both lean toward more checks, and both are one list in the table.
- Payer-written cues move credits: a payer who writes REFUND or TO SAVINGS in a reference takes their credit out of trading receipts or into the queue. Every cue is non-trading, so this can only lower M2.
- A case folder on a network share is unsupported and not detected (revision 5). A firm that redirects Documents to a server, and an underwriter who points CMA_HOME there, could let two CaseLog objects in one process both write. The default root is under the working directory, the README and the trial sheet say to keep cases on a local disk, and the owner is asked whether to detect shares.
- While another writer holds a case, a reader cannot tell a torn entry from a live append and shows an append in progress for that moment (revision 5). It never shows the entry as complete, and sign-off, which decides holding the case, always sees it.
- An outcome on screen can be read as the tool deciding. The friend said they don't want that, and the approver may object to it. The label sits beside every outcome, the recommendation stays blank, and show_policy_verdict turns the outcome off without a release.
- On the owner's own statements the illustrative policy will probably refer or fail, because a personal account has little trading income. The demo banner (R48) says the case demonstrates the mechanism; the presenter should say why before showing it.
- The categoriser still reads payer-written text, and a payer can name themselves after an acquirer. Indicators reduce this but do not remove it.
- The requirement most likely to be wrong is that the reply's 13 generic fields are the firm's memo. It is cheap to check (E8: a redacted template) and cheap to fix, because the field map is data. Next most likely: the cash default (D31) and its limits (D35), which rest on one anecdotal source; and the category order inside a bucket (D61), which is one list in the table.

### Perspective check

- **Security.** *Who can call it:* the operating-system user who owns the case root. The workspace also needs a per-launch token and a loopback Host header (R33). The case log's writer lock orders honest writers and is not access control. *Trust boundary:* imported files and payer text are checked by the loader and escaped on output (R34). *Secrets:* only an optional model key in the environment, never logged. *Personal data:* kept under one case root readable only by its owner, never committed to git (R31), never in prompts (R30); evidence holds only hashes of the profile. *Risk path:* lending data; the human acknowledgement is the named sign-off bound to a snapshot that includes the policy hash and whether the check was shown (R22). The illustrative outcome is not an acknowledgement. *Worst case:* see Security notes, now including cash paid in to look like trading, wording that mixes lists, a listed name hidden inside a longer word, and a log rewritten together with its head record.
- **Platform.** *Blast radius:* one Python package; new optional parts; the evidence JSON gains fields; PolicyResult and the checkpoint allow-list do not change. *Old and new side by side:* `cma run` is untouched, new commands are additive, and CMA_RULESET=v1 reproduces today's categories, cash included, with first-match order and no ambiguity verdict. *Resources:* files on disk only, including each case log's head record and lock file; no tables, queues, cron jobs or IAM. *Rollback:* an environment flag (CMA_RULESET), a config flip (show_policy_verdict) or a reinstall, with no data repair. *Observability:* see that section.
- **Staff.** *Smallest change:* Tier 1 is the smallest set that shows the mechanism in the reply's terms; Tier 2 can be cut off. The cash change is one category mapped into an existing set, and the policy check reuses policy.evaluate and the guard as they are. The ambiguity policy replaces per-pair exceptions with one verdict and keeps revision 2's precedence, inserting that verdict between the profile and firm rules. Revision 4 made R4 smaller: the queue it named belongs to R15. Revision 5 changes no behaviour: it writes down the case log's scope, which let D63 remove the fork machinery. *Ownership:* arithmetic and the queue's assembly stay in metrics.py and indicators.py, the outcome in policy.py, categorisation in categorise.py plus the rule data, the log and its lock in case.py; the workspace and pack only render. *Six-month misunderstandings removed:* M16 is renamed so it cannot be confused with M26 (unexplained); M18 is the cash inside M2, not a bucket beside it; [R1] to [R9] in a pack are policy rules, not requirement ids; the action reads 'sign off figures', not 'approve the loan'; OTHER means unexplained; an ambiguous credit is unexplained because the lists disagree, not because nothing matched, and its rule says conflict and names every rule it matched in either stage, not only the stage that decided; the category inside a bucket comes from the table's declared order, not rule order, while a debit still takes the first matching rule; a combining mark is part of a word; the case log separates writers only on a local disk and in processes that do not fork, so a forking server or a network share is outside it; an append in progress is not an intact log; pseudonymised data is still personal data. *Most likely wrong:* the memo fields (E8), the cash default (D31) and limits (D35), the category order inside a bucket, and the assumption that the firm keeps working files on a local disk. *Out of scope:* listed above.
- **Clean code.** *What changes most:* the rule lists, the category order and the thresholds, kept apart as data. *Names:* follow the code: money in (inflows in metrics.py), trading receipts (M2 keeps its name, Total revenue, so signed memos still read), override, firm rule, case, preview, pack, sign-off, policy check, line-type cue, name list, conflict, candidates, case-log writer, append in progress. *Tests:* properties P1-P20, synthetic truth for every bucket and indicator, golden files for the importers, a pairwise matrix over every two credit rules, a shuffle test on rule order within each stage that recategorises whole statements and compares credit categories and the money-in figures, and the case log's exclusion and reader cases run as P15 tests. *Duplication removed:* all importers share one balance walk; sign-off reuses ReviewDecision's validation; the pack's policy section reuses policy.evaluate and the guard's [Rn] check; exceptions that steer one list away from another give way to one verdict and longer phrases; rules and profile names share one whole-word definition. *Duplication introduced:* the v1 rule table, kept as data for one release. *No single-caller abstraction:* no storage interface for a future hosted version; no shared-handle registry for the case log; the label is one constant, not a templating layer; the importer contract has four callers; the rule-to-category lookup the queue uses is the table itself.
- **Data-intensive.** *Idempotency:* imports are keyed by file hash, an identical override changes nothing, and a second sign-off is refused. *Determinism:* a credit's verdict, category and candidates are a pure function of its description, the sign of its amount and the tables with their category order; neither a credit's bucket nor its category depends on rule order within a stage, and only which of several same-category rules is recorded, and the order candidates are written in, follow table order. A debit's category follows table order by design. *Ordering and concurrency:* one writer at a time per case, across windows, commands, threads and CaseLog objects in one process, on a local disk; enforced by a lock plus the expected log position; a second tab is refused while the first writes, and gets a conflict and reloads if it writes from a stale position. A reader shows an append in progress only while another writer holds the case. *Partial failure:* append-only logs with a check for a torn last entry; the pack is written to a temporary folder and then renamed. *Consistency:* the workspace reads the latest state; the preview and sign-off bind to a snapshot that includes the ruleset, thresholds, the policy hash and the show_policy_verdict setting, so a sign-off cannot attach to an outcome the underwriter did not see. *Migration:* additive, nothing to backfill. *Money:* integer pence, GBP only, thresholds compared in pence, and amounts with more than two decimals are refused.

## Data changes

**Category enum (src/cma/models.py).** Six values are added and none of the existing values change. Credit categories map to buckets as follows:

| Credit category | Bucket |
|---|---|
| revenue_card_settlement, revenue_transfer | trading receipts (M2) |
| cash_deposit (new) | trading receipts (M2); also counted in cash deposits within trading receipts (M18) |
| own_account_transfer (new; also used for debits) | own-account transfers in (M19) |
| director_funding (new) | director and shareholder funding (M20) |
| loan_drawdown | lending drawdowns (M21) |
| refund_in (new), returned_dd | refunds and returned payments in (M22) |
| hmrc_repayment (new) | HMRC repayments (M23) |
| other_explained_in (new: grants, insurance claims, interest) | other explained money in (M24) |
| other | unexplained money in (M25), ambiguous credits included |

cash_deposit joins REVENUE_CATEGORIES, so M2, M3, M4, M9, M10, M28 and the monthly trading receipts include cash. M15 does not, because it reads named customer transfers only. Under v1 no line is ever categorised cash_deposit, so v1 figures do not move. In the code and the UI, `other` now means unexplained. Merchant cash advances use loan_drawdown and loan_repayment. Gambling and cash withdrawals are read by indicators, not categories. A credit in any other category is a reconciliation failure (R3). Where a bucket holds two or three categories, the table's declared category order picks one (C1, step 6).

**Transaction (src/cma/models.py).** Three optional fields are added: the account key (default 'main'), the id of the rule, firm rule or override that set the category, and the provider's category as an untrusted hint. Amount and balance keep their current types where the model meets the rest of the code; the figures layer converts them to pence.

**Line provenance.** The rule field holds `<ruleset>:<rule id>`, `<ruleset>:default`, `<ruleset>:round_trip`, `profile:director`, `profile:own_account`, `firm:<rule id>`, or the override's reference. An ambiguous credit holds `<ruleset>:conflict:` followed by every candidate rule id in table order, joined by +, and its category is other (unexplained). Since revision 4 the candidates are every rule the credit matched in either stage, the payment rail included, after a match inside a longer listed phrase counts as the longer one; since revision 5 a phrase that starts inside another match of the same rule is found too. They form a set; table order only fixes how they are written. A credit that an override, a profile name or a pairing settles carries the rule of what settled it, as any other line does; its candidates can be recomputed from its description and the ruleset. The ruleset is always the text before the first colon, and the queue looks up each candidate's category in that ruleset.

**New figures.** No id is ever reused. M1-M16 keep their ids and units. M16 is renamed 'Non-trading money in (share)'; M2 keeps the name Total revenue, and its method sentence now names cash paid in. Values move only on lines a v2 rule now recognises or finds ambiguous (R10).

| id | name | unit |
|---|---|---|
| M17 | Total money in | GBP |
| M18 | Cash deposits within trading receipts | GBP |
| M19 | Own-account transfers in | GBP |
| M20 | Director and shareholder funding | GBP |
| M21 | Lending drawdowns | GBP |
| M22 | Refunds and returned payments in | GBP |
| M23 | HMRC repayments | GBP |
| M24 | Other explained money in | GBP |
| M25 | Unexplained money in | GBP |

Reconciliation: M17 = M2 + M19 + M20 + M21 + M22 + M23 + M24 + M25, in pence. M18 is part of M2, is not added again, and its evidence is a subset of M2's. M16 and M26 share one denominator: money in less lending drawdowns, returned direct debits and own-account transfers. Cash deposits are trading receipts, so they are in neither numerator; an ambiguous credit is unexplained, so it is in both. Credits that fall to OTHER today keep the same treatment in M9 whichever new bucket they land in, with one exception: cash deposits now count as trading cash (D31). No committed fixture or synthetic case has a cash line (Consumer trace, item 13), so no committed value moves because of cash. M27 averages the closing balance of every calendar day in the period, carried over days with no lines, as M6 does. M28 adds the proposed monthly repayment for each month of the period to existing debt service.

**Monthly summary.** One row per calendar month: the month, a part-month flag, money in, money out, trading receipts (cash included), average and lowest end-of-day balance, and days overdrawn. Each cell carries its evidence ids and their total.

**Judgement queue.** One entry per line, built by C2 and read by C5 and `cma report`: the line id, the reason it is listed (unexplained, ambiguous, possible own-account transfer, or the indicators that flagged it), and for an ambiguous credit its candidates, each with its rule id, the category that rule sets and the bucket that category maps to (none for the rail). Ordered by amount, largest first. It holds no money arithmetic.

**Indicators.** Each has an id, a kind, the threshold that fired, evidence ids and a one-line reason. Thresholds live in a packaged, validated YAML file that the firm can replace. Amounts in it are pounds with at most two decimals and are compared in pence. While a threshold still has its shipped value, the workspace and the pack call it illustrative, as they do the policy. Kinds: a large single credit against typical trading receipts; a cash deposit at or above the single-deposit limit; a month with cash above a set share of its trading receipts; cash paid in within a window of days after a cash withdrawal, the two amounts within a tolerance; a payment to a gambling operator; a returned direct debit; a run of consecutive overdrawn days; an irregular credit from a named acquirer, read from the acquirer and gateway name lists whatever category the line took. Shipped cash values: a single deposit of £2,500 or more and cash above 50% of a month's trading receipts (D35), and a round trip within 7 days and 10% of the amount (design default). An ambiguous credit is not categorised as cash, so the cash indicators do not read it; it is in the judgement queue as unexplained.

**Policy check.** PolicyResult and policy.evaluate do not change. The workspace and the pack evaluate only the packaged policy. The label is one constant with no digits in it, so it never trips the guard: 'Illustrative thresholds shipped with this tool. They are not your firm's lending policy, and this is not a lending decision: the underwriter decides.' The pack's evidence records whether the check was shown, the label, and the policy's name and hash. These do not go into PolicyResult, so the checkpoint allow-list and `cma run` are untouched. As in revision 1, the description of policy rule R9 is reworded for M16's new name, and the policy hash changes.

**Rule tables.** Packaged YAML under src/cma/rules/. v1 is today's table, frozen and pinned by hash. v2 adds acquirers, gateways, marketplaces and delivery platforms; lenders and merchant cash advance providers; move wording for own pots, spaces and savings; cash paid in (counter, deposit machine, Post Office and PayPoint); cash-withdrawal wording for the round-trip indicator only; HMRC repayments; refunds; explained non-trading credits; and the payment rail as a cue with no category. Every v2 rule carries a stage (line_type or names) and whether it is protected, and line-type rules come before names. Protected in v2: the line-type cues, and lender, director, capital-introduced, shareholder and generic loan wording. Not protected: cash paid in, the trading lists, invoice references and explained non-trading words. Compound wording lives as longer phrases on one list (STRIPE CAPITAL with the lenders, DIRECTOR LOAN with the director wording, HMRC REFUND with HMRC), not as exceptions in another list. Bare pot or savings labels and bare CAPITAL are on no list (D48). v2 also declares its category order inside each bucket that has more than one category (C1, step 6); the order is part of the table and of its hash. Tables are validated on load like the policy, and each has an id and a hash.

**Case root.** Defaults to private/cma under the working directory, or CMA_HOME, on a local disk (R42 scope). Layout:

    firm.yaml            offline_lock, show_policy_verdict, paths
    firm_rules.yaml      promoted rules: pattern, direction, category, by, at, reason
    indicators.yaml      optional firm thresholds
    memo_fields.yaml     optional firm field map
    purge_ledger.jsonl   case id, time, who
    cases/<random id>/
      case.yaml          profile: label, demo flag, business name, directors and
                         shareholders, own accounts, VAT stagger, proposed monthly repayment
      inputs/            original files, read-only, named by hash
      statements/        five-column files the loader reads, one per account, plus hint files
      overrides.jsonl    append-only
      log.jsonl          append-only, hash-chained
      log.jsonl.head     the head the log records as complete: entry count and every entry's hash
      log.jsonl.lock     the single-writer lock file; holds no data
      packs/<snapshot>/  written once at sign-off: memo.md, record.html, evidence.json, monthly.csv

Case ids are random and never the applicant's name. The profile never leaves the case folder; evidence records carry only its hash.

**Evidence JSON v1.** Everything in today's record, with its policy key unchanged. Added: schema_version, case id, accounts (key, source format, file hash, period, line count), ruleset id and hash, firm rules hash, thresholds hash, profile hash, each line's category and rule (the conflict form with every candidate included), overrides (sequence number, line, old and new category, reason, name, OS user, time), money-in buckets, monthly summary, indicators with the thresholds that fired, policy_check (shown or hidden, the label, the policy name and hash), snapshot hash, sign-off (name, OS user, time, notes), egress (offline, or the model's name) and package version.

**Synthetic truth (src/cma/synth.py).** New opt-in generator features, with truth fields for each bucket, for own-account pairs, for each line's category and for each indicator flag. Cash deposits are among them: at least one at or above the single-deposit limit and one paid in after a similar withdrawal, with truth bucket trading receipts. A new feature's description carries wording from one list only, unless its truth is unexplained with its candidates. Existing archetypes and profiles produce byte-identical statements. In their truth files, the director capital line's category becomes director_funding.

## Interfaces and contracts

**CLI (all additions)**
- `cma case new [--demo] [--label TEXT]` prints a random case id.
- `cma case import CASE FILE --format csv|monzo|openbanking|truelayer|bank-csv[:PROFILE] [--account KEY] [--statement-balance AMOUNT --at DATE]`
- `cma case profile CASE` edits the profile.
- `cma case check CASE` checks the case log against its head record: exit 0 when it is intact, noting an append in progress while another writer holds the case; exit 3 naming the first bad entry, by number only.
- `cma review CASE` starts the workspace, prints the URL with its token, and opens a browser if there is one.
- `cma case preview CASE` prints the memo sections, the policy check when it is shown, and the snapshot hash. It writes nothing.
- `cma case sign CASE` asks for a typed name, checks the snapshot against the preview and writes the pack once. It is interactive only; there is no --yes. Preview and sign-off are also available in the workspace.
- `cma case purge CASE`
- `cma verify EVIDENCE [--case CASE]`
- `cma pseudonymise FILE --format F --out PATH [--profile CASE]`
- `cma report` prints a summary of every case with no personal data and no durations.
- `cma generate` gains opt-in features: director funding, own-account transfers, cash (with large deposits and a round trip), real acquirers, lenders, two accounts.
- Environment variables: CMA_HOME (case root, on a local disk) and CMA_RULESET (v1 or v2).

**Importer contract.** Input: one source file and an optional statement balance. Output: the five-column statement for one account, the account metadata, and an optional hints file. The output must pass ingest.load_csv unchanged, and all importers share one step that walks and verifies the balance. Refusals give line or record numbers, never cell values, as the MCP server does today.

**Case log contract** (revision 5; internal to the package, with the writers listed in Consumer trace item 20). A writer appends inside the case's writer lock and states the log position it last saw; it is refused at once while another writer holds the case, and gets a conflict if its position is stale. A reader gets the log's head and entries, the first bad entry with its reason, or the recorded entries with an append in progress. Messages name entries by number and never quote their content. No caller forks while it holds the lock, and every caller keeps case folders on a local disk.

**Review workspace API.** Internal to the package; the page and the server ship together, so it is not a shared contract. It can read the case summary and the judgement queue; read the lines behind a figure, bucket, monthly cell or indicator; read the policy check when it is shown; post an override with the expected log position; preview and promote a firm rule; build a preview; sign off a preview's snapshot; and post a comparison figure. A line in the judgement queue carries its rule and, for an ambiguous credit, its candidates, each with the category its rule sets and the bucket that category maps to, or no bucket for the rail (R15). A line in a drill-down carries its rule as recorded (R16). The firm-rule preview counts the lines the rule matches and will not apply to, because a protected or ambiguous verdict settled them (R20, R21, R4). Every request carries the session token. Every write carries the expected log position, gets a conflict if that position is stale, and is refused while another writer holds the case.

**Pack.** memo.md has the sections in field-map order, figures with their ids, and blanks left for the underwriter. While the check is shown, it then has a 'Policy check (illustrative)' section that no memo field maps to. Its first line is the label, followed by the overall outcome and each rule, with the metric value tagged [Mn] and the threshold tagged [Rn] after a limit word. record.html is self-contained and makes no external requests; it shows the label wherever an outcome appears, and shows the sign-off. The pack also holds evidence.json (v1) and monthly.csv. It is written once, at sign-off, to a new folder named by the snapshot hash.

**Memo field map.** A YAML file giving, for each field, its name, the figures or tables it shows, and a sentence template whose numbers are placeholders for figure ids. Fields with nothing mapped are marked for the underwriter. The default reproduces the 13 fields in the reply: business details, loan request, trading history, monthly turnover and credits, average and minimum balances, existing commitments, returned direct debits, VAT and HMRC, director loans and transfers between the business's accounts, credit history, affordability, key risks and recommendation. The turnover field states the cash within trading receipts (M18) next to M2. Credit history, key risks and recommendation stay blank. The policy check is not a memo field; the recommendation stays blank whatever the outcome.

**MCP tools.** compute_metrics returns M1-M28 (an addition), and a new read-only tool returns the money-in buckets. check_policy is unchanged. The server refuses to start under the offline lock.

**Graph and prompt.** Unchanged, except that the metrics table has more rows and M16 has its new name. FakeLLM parses rows by id and is unaffected; its sentence about M16 is reworded. Under v2 the evidence lines `cma run` writes carry each line's rule, the conflict form included; the terminal review shows no queue.

The CSV is byte-identical; the appendix gains M17-M28, M16's new name and M2's reworded method; in the truth file, the director capital line becomes director_funding. test_categoriser_matches_truth runs under v2; v1 is covered by P7, which compares values.

Cash paid in loses protected; the bare pot label rule goes; exceptions that hide another list's cue go (the HMRC rule's savings exception), and longer phrases join the lists instead; the conflict check covers names in any two buckets. v2 is not yet the default and has never written a signed pack, so no evidence record needs repair.

## Rollout and flag

**Flags**
- CMA_RULESET: becomes v2 by default once evals, P7, the pairwise matrix and the rule-order shuffle pass; v1 restores today's categorisation exactly, including cash falling to OTHER and first-match order.

**Steps**
1. Land C1 (tables as data, v1 frozen) with P7 passing before any category changes.
2. Land the v2 tables (cash wording and the category order included) and the two-stage verdict with the ambiguity policy, then the buckets, monthly summary, indicators and judgement queue, behind CMA_RULESET. Make v2 the default when evals, the pairwise matrix, the rule-order shuffle (over whole statements, comparing every credit's category and the money-in figures, round-trip pass included) and truth for the new synthetic features pass, the cash truth included.

## Observability

Nothing leaves the machine, so every signal is local, and the underwriter chooses whether to share it.

- **The signal that proves it works:** for each case, in `cma report` and in each pack's evidence: traced figures 100% (every figure has evidence or an n/a reason), reconciled true, guard grounded true.
- **The signal that tests the bet's biggest risk:** M26 (unexplained share of money in) on each real or pseudonymised case, and the size of the judgement queue. If most cases sit above 20%, the tool is handing the hard part back (board risk 1).
- **The signal that tests the ambiguity policy (D52, D60):** per case, the number of ambiguous credits, their share of money in, and their candidate sets, read from line provenance in each pack's evidence and counted among the judgement-queue lines in `cma report`. A candidate set that keeps recurring, and that the underwriter keeps moving the same way, needs a longer phrase on one list, not a rule order.
- **The signal that tests the category order (revision 4):** overrides between two categories of the same bucket (customer transfer to card settlement, returned payment to refund), counted by kind in `cma report`. If the friend keeps reversing the order's choice, the order is wrong.
- **The signal that tests D31:** overrides from cash_deposit to another category, and cash deposits flagged, per case, in `cma report`. If the friend moves most flagged cash out of trading receipts, the default or the limits are wrong.
- **The signal for the case log (revision 5):** `cma case check` per case: intact, intact with an append in progress, or the first bad entry by number. A writer refused because another holds the case says so ('another window or command is writing to this case') and writes nothing. If the friend sees that message when only one window is open, something outside the scope (a share, a forked process) is writing.
- **The signal for the outcome:** stopwatch minutes per file, recorded by the friend on the trial sheet outside the tool (D32). The case log keeps event times for the audit trail only, and nothing reports a duration from them.
- **Log lines:** one structured line per event in the case log, with the event, case id, snapshot and counts, and no names or descriptions. For example: event=pack_written traced=100 reconciled=true unexplained_pct=12.4 ambiguous=4 cash_flagged=3 policy_check=shown.
- **What fires when it does not work:** there is no remote alerting, by design. Instead the tool refuses, with a non-zero exit and a red banner, when reconciliation fails, the guard fails, the log chain breaks, or a hosted model is requested under the offline lock. `cma report` exits non-zero if any case has traced figures below 100% or reconciled false, and `cma case check` exits 3 on a broken log.
- **CI:** evals extended with truth for every bucket and indicator, the cash ones included; P1-P20 run as tests, with the pairwise matrix, the rule-order shuffle over whole statements, and the case log's exclusion and reader cases marked as P15 tests; the wheel check also loads the rule tables, thresholds, field map and packaged policy from an installed wheel.

## Security notes

- **Who can call it:** the operating-system user who owns the case root, whose files are readable by that user only. The workspace listens only on loopback, requires a random per-launch token on every request, rejects any Host header that is not the loopback address (against DNS rebinding) and sends no permissive CORS headers. Writes also need the expected log position.
- **The writer lock is not access control** (revision 5). It orders honest writers on one machine; access is the case root's owner-only permissions (R31). Someone with the operating-system user's rights can rewrite the log and its head record together, which the files alone cannot show. The sign-off and the pack pin the log head they saw (R22), so a later rewrite no longer matches a signed pack.
- **Trust boundary:** bank data the applicant controls enters through the importers, and the loader checks its structure and balance chain. Descriptions are written by payers: they are escaped in HTML under a strict content security policy with no inline script, formula characters are neutralised in CSV, and they never go into prompts. The candidate rule ids shown for an ambiguous credit, and their buckets, come from the shipped tables, not from payer text. Case-log messages name entries by number and never quote their content.
- **Secrets:** an optional model API key in the environment, only when the offline lock is off. It is never logged or written to evidence.
- **Personal data:** statements, the profile (director names, account identifiers), override reasons and packs stay in the case folder. Evidence holds ids, figures, rule ids and hashes, not descriptions or the profile. Purge deletes the folder and leaves a ledger line with no personal data. Encryption at rest is the firm's disk encryption; the tool adds none of its own. Retention follows the firm's policy, and purge is how the tool carries it out.
- **Risk path:** lending data (risk: data). The human acknowledgement is the named sign-off bound to the snapshot. The name is typed, not authenticated (README known limit), so the OS user is recorded alongside it. Real sign-in belongs to a hosted version. The illustrative outcome is computed, labelled and never signed as a decision.
- **Worst cases with this deployed:** First, an applicant writes script into a payment reference to act in the underwriter's browser, for example to change categories or sign off. Escaping, the content security policy and the token stop it. Second, an applicant makes money look like trading income: by naming the payer after an acquirer, adding an invoice reference, or, since D31, paying cash in. The round-trip check, the director-name rule and the indicators surface it, including the three cash indicators, and the line is one click from its figure. Cash split below every limit is the gap that remains, and it is visible only in M18. Since D52, wording that mixes lists cannot reach trading receipts: a payer who adds a second list's word only sends the credit to the queue, and every line-type cue is non-trading, so payer-written cues can lower M2 but never raise it. Since revision 4, a listed name hidden inside a longer word in any alphabet does not match, and since revision 5 that holds after a combining mark too; adding a gateway's name to an invoice-referenced credit does not take it out of the round-trip check. Third, someone turns the offline lock off and uses a hosted model. The prompt still carries only tables (P5), and the evidence records where data went. Fourth, someone with the underwriter's login edits the case log and its head record together to hide an override; the files cannot show it, but a signed pack pins the head it saw, so the edit shows against every signed pack.
- **Pseudonymised is not anonymous:** amounts, dates and balances can identify a business again, so the output is labelled as personal data.
