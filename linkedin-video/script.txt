A week ago my friend, an underwriter, cancelled our road trip. He was buried in bank statements.
His fear about AI wasn't speed. It was this: "If it makes up a number, it's my name on the file."
So I started building a tool for him with Bridgie, my AI agent team.
First, Bridgie interviewed me. Seventy-six questions. Then it wrote a spec: fifty-five requirements, twenty rules that must always hold, and forty-one tasks.
You could prompt an AI into a demo in an afternoon. But you couldn't prove a single number in it.
In Bridgie, a task isn't done when the AI says so. It passes the tests, then a reviewer from a different AI company tries to break it, then a human signs.
The statement reader alone took sixty-five rounds. It caught a fraction of a penny lost to rounding, a refund counted as income, a file that crashed instead of naming the bad line.
A week in, nineteen of forty-one are done. Statements read to the penny, every figure traceable to its transactions, nothing leaving the laptop.
Not released yet, and no underwriter has used it on real work yet.
Nineteen of forty-one isn't slow. It's what "I'd sign this" costs.
