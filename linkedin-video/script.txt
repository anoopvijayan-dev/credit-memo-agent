"If AI makes up a number, it's my name on the file."
My friend told me that. He's an underwriter. He'd just cancelled our road trip, buried in bank statements.
So I'm building him a tool. Not with one AI. With a team of them. I call it Bridgie.
Before any code, Bridgie interviewed me. Seventy-six questions. Forty-one tasks.
Any AI can build you a demo in an afternoon. None of them can prove a single number in it.
So in Bridgie, the AI doesn't get to say "done".
The tests decide. Then a reviewer from a different AI company tries to break it. Then a human signs.
The statement reader went through sixty-five rounds of review before it passed.
It caught a fraction of a penny lost to rounding. A refund counted as income. A file that crashed instead of naming the bad line.
The kind of bug that ends up with his name on it.
One week in: nineteen of forty-one done. Read to the penny. Every figure traced to its transactions. Nothing leaves the laptop.
Not released yet. No underwriter has used it on real work yet.
Nineteen of forty-one isn't slow. It's what "I'd sign this" costs.
